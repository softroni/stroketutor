Design handbook · v3

This is the third design of the StrokeTutor iOS learner app: the same product, the same flows and the same rules as the first document, with a new visual system built to stand next to Duolingo and Simply Draw on a home screen. White paper, one bold green, tactile buttons, a visible path of lesson nodes, bigger type. Every screen here is interactive: Continue walks through onboarding, a node opens its lesson, Start drawing opens the player, I drew it moves it on.

What does not change

  • The ten rules of the first handbook: watch → draw → confirm → continue; the paper is the product; adult tone; reality stays in view; one loud button; big and readable; paths unlock in order; calm progress; local and private; the existing player is kept and wrapped.
  • Narration and Lina, as specified in the first document (voice pipeline, playback rules, bounded use of the character).
  • The app map, the on-device data model, the content files the app reads, the accessibility checklist and the milestone mapping. Open questions carry over.
  • Screen ids and flow links, so the two documents can be read side by side: index.html#hp-home and v3.html#hp-home are the same screen in two designs.

Implementation notes for SwiftUI

  • Pressed edge. A ButtonStyle that draws a rounded rectangle offset 4 pt below the label in the edge colour and moves the label down 4 pt while isPressed. One style, three colour sets (green, white, ink).
  • Nodes. LessonNode(state:, drawing:): a 84 pt circle, the lesson's finished drawing rendered from its tutorial JSON at 60 pt (white strokes on gold when done), the halo as a scaling Circle().stroke with .repeatForever unless Reduce Motion.
  • The zig-zag. A VStack of rows, each offset by an alternating x in [−54, 54] pt, label on the outer side. Keep the tap target the node itself.
  • White paper. tutorial.style.backgroundColor stays in the data; the player ignores it in favour of Theme.paper = .white unless the lesson sets an explicit colour (colour-fill lessons in v2 schema).
  • Hero banner. A LinearGradient green card with a 6 pt edge; the drawing thumbnail is a white rounded rectangle with the tutorial rendered into it.

Tap the screen to walk into the flow. The six strokes are the real Simple House tutorial, drawn fast, with a green pen tip riding each line.

Developer notes

Purpose

Cover the launch gap with the product itself: a drawing appearing on white paper. No gradient, no logo lockup.

Layout

  • Page --page (white). A 292 pt square drawing area centred between two spacers, the 40/800 wordmark 4 pt under it, one 17 pt muted line.
  • Strokes at 22 (walls, roof) and 16 (the rest) in a 1000-unit canvas, i.e. ~6 pt and ~4.5 pt lines: the launch drawing is bolder than a lesson thumbnail on purpose.
  • The pen tip is a 16-unit --green dot that follows the active stroke and vanishes when the stroke ends (offset-path here; .trim + a point along the path in SwiftUI).

States & interactions

  • LaunchScreen.storyboard shows the finished house and the wordmark, static. SplashView is laid out identically and redraws the six strokes in 3.7 s (0.25 → 3.7 s). Strokes play strictly in sequence, as in the player.
  • Hold at least 0.9 s; go on as soon as the catalog is ready after that, mid-drawing if need be. hasCompletedOnboarding == false → ob-1, else hp-home. No tap is needed in the app; the mockup body is tappable so the document can be clicked through.

What changed in v3

  • White instead of cream paper; the whole house instead of a single swash line — the finished drawing is now the app's signature everywhere (nodes, thumbs), so launch shows the same object.
  • A green pen tip: green is the way forward throughout v3, and the tip is what the learner will follow in the player. §31

SwiftUI mapping

SplashView: ZStack { TutorialStrokes(.simpleHouse, animated: !reduceMotion, tip: .green) }
  .frame(width: 292, height: 292)
  .task { try? await Task.sleep(for: .seconds(0.9)); route() }

Accessibility

  • One VoiceOver element, "StrokeTutor", header trait; the drawing is hidden.
  • Reduce Motion: strokes appear complete with a 0.2 s fade; no pen tip. Wordmark is a vector image so the storyboard is crisp at every scale.

Open questions

  • Animate on every cold launch or only the first of the day? Proposal: first launch of the day, static otherwise.

Developer notes

Purpose

The single most important idea in the product, §4 "Watch → Draw → Confirm → Continue", shown as three tiles so it is grasped in one glance and remembered as a rhythm.

Layout

  • Rail at 33 %. Lina lina--md (pose pen) with the bubble.
  • Three .ob-tiles: 22 pt radius, --surface fill, 12 pt gap; an 88 pt white paper art on the left, then eyebrow 12/800 green ("1 · Watch"), headline 17/700, footnote 13/600 muted.
  • Tile 3's art is the player's own .btn-primary at 76×40 pt — the learner should recognise the control on sight in the first lesson.
  • One 17 pt muted line under the tiles states the promise; 64 pt Continue pinned.

States & interactions

  • Tile 1: walls then roof, real tutorial strokes at 44 units. Tile 2: a slightly wobbly square (the learner's hand, not the app's) draws at 3.0 s, the pen badge pops at 2.9 s. Tile 3: the button presses (4 pt down, edge to 0) at 5.6 s for 0.28 s. Then everything holds.
  • Continue is live from the first frame; nothing gates on the animation.

What changed in v3

  • Three soft tiles replace v1's interactive two-step demo (beat 5) and its triptych (beat 4): shorter flow, one memorable image per idea. Duolingo for the tile rhythm, §30 for the copy.
  • Tactile mini button, white paper thumbs with 2 pt borders, no outlined cards.

SwiftUI mapping

VStack(spacing: 12) {
  MethodTile("1 · Watch", "One step draws itself.", "Then it stops and waits.") { TutorialStrokes(.simpleHouse, steps: ["walls","roof"], delay: 0.4) }
  MethodTile("2 · Draw",  "You copy it on paper.",  "Take as long as you like.") { HandSquare(delay: 3.0) }
  MethodTile("3 · Tap",   "Tap \"I drew it\".",     "The next step begins.")    { BigPrimaryButton.miniature.pressed(at: 5.6) }
}

Accessibility

  • Each tile is one element: "1, Watch. One step draws itself. Then it stops and waits." Art is hidden. Order: rail → bubble → tiles → promise → Continue.
  • Dynamic Type: tiles grow vertically, the art stays 88 pt; at accessibility sizes the body scrolls under the pinned button.
  • Reduce Motion: all three arts render complete; no press.

Open questions

  • Should tile 3 be tappable and actually press (a rehearsal), or does a tappable thing inside a beat invite the wrong habit? Recommend not tappable.

Purpose

One question, one answer: what do I draw next? The hero answers it in one tap; the path underneath shows where it leads §29. No streaks, XP or goals — nothing on this screen can go down §30.

Layout

  • Path header: eyebrow "Houses · 2 of 10 drawn" and a blue text link "All paths" → hp-paths.

  • Path finished (not drawn): the hero reads "You have drawn every house in this path" with Choose another path → hp-paths; see hp-path variant complete.

What changed in v3

  • White page; the hero is a filled green banner instead of a bordered card, with a white thumbnail and a white-on-green button Duolingo.
  • The path cards and "More paths" button are gone: the current path is drawn as nodes on the home screen, so progress is seen as drawings rather than read as "2 of 10". Other paths sit behind one text link.
  • Type up a size everywhere (title 36, hero title 24, labels 16); tactile 4 pt-edge buttons; gold = drawn, green = next, grey = later.
