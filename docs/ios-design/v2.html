Developer handbook

This document is the layout and behaviour specification for the Paper Coach iOS learner app: every screen, every state, and the notes an engineer needs to build it in SwiftUI. It was designed from the master plan (docs/StrokeTutor_Master_Plan.pdf, Part VI §29–§32 and Part I §1–§7), from the player that already exists in StrokeTutor/, and from Duolingo's onboarding, home and completion patterns on Mobbin, used for craft only. Nothing in StrokeTutor/, web/ or shared/ was changed to make it.

How to read this document

  • One screen mode (default) shows a screen with its developer notes. Gallery shows every phone at once. ← → step through screens.
  • Buttons inside the phones are live where a flow exists: Continue in onboarding walks to the next beat, a lesson card opens its preview, Start drawing opens the player, I drew it moves the player to the next state, the tab bar switches tabs. State chips above a phone switch its variants.
  • Phones are iPhone 16 Pro logical size (393 × 852 pt, 1 CSS px = 1 pt). iPad variants are shown scaled.
  • Source: docs/ios-design/src/. node docs/ios-design/build.mjs rebuilds index.html; --check validates the fragments. Screens are in src/screens/, one file per group; the design tokens are src/design-system.css; the tutor and icons are src/symbols.svg.html.

Ten rules the whole app follows

  1. Watch → draw → confirm → continue. A step animates, stops, and waits. Nothing advances without I drew it. No timers, no countdowns, no auto-advance (§4, §31).
  2. The paper is the product. There is no digital canvas. The phone demonstrates; the learner draws on real paper; the sketchbook keeps a photo of the page (§7, §32).
  3. Adult tone. "Nice work. Your cottage is finished." Never "AMAZING!!! +500 XP". No scores, grades, XP, likes, leaderboards or streak pressure (§2, §30).
  4. Reality stays in view. The reference photo is on the preview, a persistent thumbnail in the player, side by side on iPad (§5, §31).
  5. One loud button. Green is reserved for the action that moves the learner forward. Everything else is white, outlined or plain text.
  6. Big and readable. Body 17 pt minimum, instruction 22 pt, tap targets 60 pt (Theme.minimumTapTarget), rounded SF type, high contrast (§31).
  7. Paths unlock in order; paths switch freely. Progress is per path and never lost by switching (§6).
  8. Calm progress. Progress is finished drawings: "3 of 10 drawn", a sketchbook page count. Not points.
  9. Local and private. No account, no cloud, no analytics by default. Everything lives on the device (§32, §38).
  10. The existing player is kept. PlayerViewModel, DrawingCanvasView, StepInstructionCard, Theme and the lenient loader stay; M7 wraps them in a product shell.

Two additions that are not in the master plan

Narration: the tutor's voice on every step

Every lesson step is narrated: a short recorded sentence that tells the learner how to make the stroke while it draws, the way the Simply Draw app does. The written instruction stays on screen at all times; audio is never the only channel. Narration plays from the start of a step's animation, is replayed together with the animation by Watch again, ignores the speed setting, respects the silent switch, ducks other audio, and can be muted with one tap (persisted in Settings). Onboarding introduces the voice once. The plan reserved step.voiceover for exactly this and deferred the decision (§39); this design takes it. Production is in the Studio with the ElevenLabs API, later; the pipeline is specified below.

Lina: a tutor you can see, within limits

The plan says to avoid mascot-driven motivation (§30). The creator wants the learner to feel that someone is teaching them. The compromise is Lina, an adult artist drawn in the product's own pen-and-ink language, whose role is bounded:

Voice pipeline (Studio → repo → app)

  1. Authoring. In the Studio, a lesson gets a Generate voice action (not built yet). For each step it sends the step's instruction — or a dedicated spoken line if the creator writes one — to the ElevenLabs text-to-speech API with the tutor's fixed voice id, and stores the result in the workspace beside the lesson. Onboarding lines and completion lines are a small fixed script, generated once.
  2. Publishing. Publish writes shared/Assets/Voice/<lessonId>/<stepId>.m4a (AAC, mono, 48 kbps, about 20–40 KB per step) and a manifest.json beside them: { voiceId, model, generatedAt, steps: { stepId: { file, text, textHash, durationMs } } }. The tutorial JSON is untouched: v1 stays frozen and voiceover stays null. The manifest's textHash lets the Studio warn when an instruction changed after its audio was made.
  3. App. NarrationPlayer looks up the manifest at lesson load; a missing manifest or file means the step plays silently and the chip is hidden. Files are bundled with the app (the whole first catalog is a few MB); on-demand resources are a later option.
  4. Playback rules (repeated in the player notes): start with beginStep; cancel on replay, back, leave; I drew it appears when the animation and the narration have both finished; speed never changes the voice; AVAudioSession category .ambient, mode .spokenAudio, options [.duckOthers, .interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers] (music ducks, podcasts pause, the silent switch is respected, narration stops on lock — decided after the player and settings notes argued both sides; the counter-argument is in the open questions); the mute toggle persists; VoiceOver users get the written instruction read by VoiceOver, so narration pauses when VoiceOver is speaking.

Accessibility checklist (§31)

  • Dynamic Type through the accessibility sizes: text grows, canvases yield height, cards scroll, buttons wrap to two lines before they truncate.
  • Reduce Motion: non-essential motion (transitions, the wave, hero animations) stops; the stroke animation is essential and stays, but the app offers a stepwise "show the whole step" alternative in Accessibility settings.
  • VoiceOver: every control has a label and a hint; the canvas is one element that describes the step ("Step 2 of 5, the roof, drawing" then "drawn"); onboarding bubbles are read before their button; photos in the sketchbook are labelled by lesson and date.
  • Contrast: ink on paper is 12:1; green on white 4.6:1 for 24 pt bold text; muted labels never go below 55 % ink.
  • Tap targets 60 pt; no hidden gestures — everything reachable by a visible button; left-handed layout mirrors the control row and the reference thumbnail corner.
  • Orientation: the player rotates on iPhone and iPad (nowhere else in the app). Portrait draws tall subjects larger, landscape wide ones; the paper is fitted to the drawing's bounds, not the full canvas, so both gain. iPad landscape puts the reference beside the paper.

What this document deliberately leaves out

  • Accounts, sign-in, cloud sync, sharing feeds, likes, leaderboards, XP, streak mechanics (§30, §32 "Do not build yet").
  • A digital drawing canvas or any brush tools.
  • AI scoring of the learner's photo.
  • A paywall or trial flow.
  • Dark appearance: the paper is light by design; the document chrome is theme-aware, the phones are not.

Open questions for the creator

Collected from every screen's notes. Each is a call the creator should make before M7 starts; none blocks the design.

Content and catalog

  • Today's simple-house.json plays in about a minute by the placeholder time formula, not the 3–5 minutes §7 promises; it also already ends with a chimney, which Appendix A reserves for lesson 2. M6 decides what Simple House is.
  • palm-tree-4 is schema v2; iOS reads v1 only, so Trees would be empty in a real build until M7 adds v2 playback. The Trees lessons 2–4 named in the mockups ("Round Tree", "Pine Tree", "Bare Winter Tree") are invented.
  • paths.json needs a description and an icon per path, and may list paths with empty lessonIds so "not yet available" stays data-driven.
  • lessons.json reference needs alt (VoiceOver), an author, and a licence enumeration instead of free text before any attribution-required photo ships.
  • Proposed nullable additions for a future schema version, ignorable by v1 players: tutorial.orientation (the one learning idea shown before step 1; the beat appears only when it is non-empty), step.detail (a one-line hint under the instruction), and a per-lesson subjectNoun for "Your house is finished" (or a generic headline that survives translation).
  • Beat 7 of onboarding shows a "Cottage With Foreground" drawing that must become a real published lesson before ship.

Player

  • The outlined "I drew it" is tappable while the step is still drawing (skip ahead). Recommendation from the player notes: ship it plain; the wording is the check. Alternative: a confirm-tap on the first three lessons.
  • The paper is fitted to the drawing's bounds (union of all strokes + 6 % margin). Should the Studio write an explicit tutorial.fit rectangle, or does the player always compute it?
  • Silent switch: respected (.ambient) in this design; .playback (speak even when silenced, as Maps does) is the alternative for a phone propped beside the paper.
  • iPhone landscape in the player is in; decide whether the iPhone SE class (375 pt tall in landscape) is in scope for it, and whether wide lessons get a "draws larger on its side" line on the preview.
  • Should the speed chosen inside a lesson persist as the default? Recommendation: persist it; it is a preference.
  • Which ElevenLabs voice is Lina, and is its commercial licence in place? Is the orientation beat narrated (a sixth line per lesson) or silent?

Completion and sketchbook

  • Is "4 min drawing time" a fact the learner wants on the completion screen, or quiet pressure? "Lesson 1 of 10" or the objective would be neutral.
  • "Also save to Photos": a checkbox on the saved screen, or Settings only (calmer)?
  • iCloud device backup carries the sketchbook; soften "stays on this device" in the privacy text, or exclude the folder from backups?
  • The "your first tree vs your latest tree" comparison is drawn as a future variant, marked so on screen; confirm it never leaves the app.

Onboarding, settings, legal

  • Beat 7 implies "in a few weeks" as the pace; is that the promise?
  • Default reminder time (19:30 proposed) and whether the reminder is offered in onboarding at all, or only in Settings.
  • Analytics: none, or local-only and opt-in? The privacy page currently promises none.
  • A real contact address and a hosted privacy-policy URL are needed for review.

Developer notes

Purpose

Cover the launch gap with something that already teaches the visual language. No logo lockup, no gradient splash: paper, ink, one line.

Layout

  • Background --paper (#FAF7F0), not --page — the launch screen is a sheet of paper.
  • Mark block centred optically: the 260×98 pen line, the 40 pt/800 wordmark, a 15 pt subhead at --ink-55. Nothing else, no version string.

States & interactions

  • Static frame = LaunchScreen.storyboard. A storyboard renders before any of our code runs, so it cannot animate and cannot read Dynamic Type. It must contain the finished line and the wordmark as a static composition (an asset-catalog image plus a label, or a single PDF vector image).
  • Animated frame = the first SwiftUI view (SplashView), laid out pixel-identically to the storyboard so the hand-off is invisible; it re-draws the same path over 2.4 s with a trim animation.
  • Minimum on-screen time 0.9 s, maximum 1.6 s; if the catalog is ready earlier we still hold to 0.9 s so it never flickers. Then: hasCompletedOnboarding == false → ob-1; otherwise → hp-home.
  • No tap is required in the real app. The mockup makes the body tappable only so the document can be clicked through.

Data

Reads one value, @AppStorage("hasCompletedOnboarding") var hasCompletedOnboarding = false. Nothing is written here.

Accessibility

  • The storyboard frame cannot scale with Dynamic Type; keep the wordmark a vector image so it is crisp at every scale and let the SwiftUI view take over before any text needs to scale.
  • VoiceOver: the whole splash is one element labelled “Paper Coach”, .accessibilityAddTraits(.isHeader); the line is .accessibilityHidden(true).
  • Reduce Motion: skip the trim animation and show the completed line with a 0.2 s opacity fade. §31

SwiftUI mapping — the flow container

Recommendation: a NavigationStack of OnboardingStep values, not a TabView(.page).

enum OnboardingStep: Int, Hashable, CaseIterable {
    case problem, blankPage, whatToNotice, fromPhoto,
         method, whatYouNeed, progress, choosePath, voice, ready
}

struct OnboardingFlow: View {
    @State private var path: [OnboardingStep] = []
    var body: some View {
        NavigationStack(path: $path) {
            BeatView(.problem, advance: advance)
                .navigationDestination(for: OnboardingStep.self) { BeatView($0, advance: advance) }
        }
        .toolbar(.hidden, for: .navigationBar)   // the rail replaces the nav bar
        .interactiveDismissDisabled()
    }
}
  • Why not TabView(.page): paging keeps neighbouring beats alive, so the stroke animations on beats 4, 5 and 7 start while the page is still off-screen and are half-finished when it arrives; a half-completed drag also desyncs the rail; and free swiping lets a learner skim past the choice on beat 8. §31 “minimise hidden gestures”
  • Why a stack: each beat is pushed fresh, so onAppear is exactly the moment the drawing should begin; back is one explicit chevron; the order is programmatic, so Skip is path = [.choosePath] and nothing else changes.
  • Keep .navigationBarBackButtonHidden(true) but leave the interactive pop gesture on — it mirrors the chevron rather than replacing it.
  • The rail is one OnboardingRail(progress:onBack:onSkip:) in a .safeAreaInset(edge: .top), so every beat body gets the same 52 pt of chrome without repeating it.

Open questions

  • Do we want the same line animation on every cold launch, or only on the first launch of a session? Repeated 2.4 s animations become a tax on a daily user — proposal: animate on first launch of the day, static otherwise.

Purpose

Beat 1 of the eight in §30. Name the wish before naming the product. No feature is mentioned on this screen and none should be.

Layout

Data

Voice & narration

Silent. Nothing in the flow plays audio before ob-voice has offered the learner the switch — the first sound the app makes must be one the learner asked for. Lina’s line for this beat is recorded all the same (see the table on ob-voice).

  • Skip is 44 pt, the rail’s established size; every decision-bearing control in the flow (Continue, path rows, toggles) is ≥ 60 pt. §2

Three white forms draw themselves over the photograph in 4 s. The learner sees simplification happen rather than reading the word “simplification”. §5 “reality → interpretation → drawing”

Developer notes

Purpose

Beat 3 §30 and the introduction of the tutor. Lina is a bounded departure from “avoid mascot-driven motivation”: she is an adult artist who teaches, introduced by name once and then mostly heard rather than seen.

Layout

  • .tutor-says: 104×124 portrait (#lina-point) + .speech bubble with the tail on the left at 26 pt from the top. Duolingo · mascot + bubble Calmer: no bounce, no eyes-follow, one sentence.
  • Photograph flexible, with an overlay SVG in the same coordinate space, strokes in white at 95 %.
  • Two 14 pt chips below, wrapping to two rows at large text sizes.

States & interactions

  • On appear: form 1 (0.3 s → 1.4 s), form 2 (1.5 s → 2.5 s), form 3 (2.6 s → 3.4 s). Then it holds; it does not loop. Returning to the beat with the back chevron replays it once.
  • Tapping the photograph replays the overlay — an undocumented nicety, never required.

Data

The three overlay paths are authored for this screen (they are not a lesson) and live in the onboarding asset, expressed in the same M/L/Z grammar as tutorial strokes so the same renderer draws them.

Voice & narration

Silent here. The bubble text is written to be identical to the recorded line, so when the narrated flow ships nothing has to be rewritten. The art for Lina is placeholder direction for an illustrator; pose set neutral / pen / point / wave.

Accessibility

  • VoiceOver order: rail → bubble (“Lina says: I’m Lina. Drawing gets easier…”) → the two chips → Continue. The portrait is decorative and hidden; the bubble always precedes the button. Set this with .accessibilitySortPriority rather than relying on layout order.
  • Dynamic Type: the bubble is the element that must grow. At dynamicTypeSize >= .accessibility1 the row becomes a column — portrait above, full-width bubble below with .speech--below tail — via ViewThatFits. The bubble never truncates and never scrolls internally.

Fitting the drawing

StrokeShape.transform(canvas:in:) already fits a rectangle into a rectangle, centred; today the source rectangle is always (0, 0, canvas.width, canvas.height). Replace the source with the lesson's stroke bounds, computed once at load:

// PreparedTutorial — computed once, constant for the whole lesson so the paper never jumps.
/// Union of every stroke's bounding box, grown by half its line width, then by 6 % of
/// the larger side on every edge. Falls back to the full canvas for a lesson with no strokes.
var fittedBounds: CGRect {
    var box: CGRect? = nil
    for step in steps { for stroke in step.strokes {
        let r = stroke.path.boundingRect.insetBy(dx: -stroke.lineWidth / 2, dy: -stroke.lineWidth / 2)
        box = box.map { $0.union(r) } ?? r
    } }
    guard let b = box, !b.isEmpty else { return CGRect(origin: .zero, size: canvas) }
    let m = max(b.width, b.height) * 0.06
    return b.insetBy(dx: -m, dy: -m)
}

// StrokeShape — `canvas: CGSize` becomes `source: CGRect`; scale and offset are unchanged in spirit.
static func transform(source: CGRect, in rect: CGRect) -> CGAffineTransform {
    let scale = min(rect.width / source.width, rect.height / source.height)
    let offsetX = rect.minX + (rect.width  - source.width  * scale) / 2 - source.minX * scale
    let offsetY = rect.minY + (rect.height - source.height * scale) / 2 - source.minY * scale
    return CGAffineTransform(translationX: offsetX, y: offsetY).scaledBy(x: scale, y: scale)
}
  • The fit uses all steps, not the steps drawn so far, so the scale is fixed for the lesson. A pencil tip, the ghost on the orientation beat and the faded earlier steps all share it.
  • Line widths scale with the drawing (they are canvas units), so a 16-unit line is 5.4 pt wide on the house instead of 3.2 pt today. That is a feature: the ink was thin.
  • The Studio can later write the same rectangle into the file (a nullable tutorial.fit) to let an author choose a looser crop; a v1 player ignores it and computes its own.
  • The web player in web/ is untouched by this document; if it adopts the same rule the two stay pixel-consistent.

Voice & narration

One recorded file per step, shared/Assets/Voice/<lessonId>/<stepId>.m4a (AAC, mono, 48 kbps — a 12 s line is ~70 KB, so all five Simple House lines ship in under 400 KB). step.voiceover in the schema holds the relative path when the Studio writes one; fall back to the convention when it is null.

@MainActor final class NarrationPlayer: NSObject, AVAudioPlayerDelegate {
    private var player: AVAudioPlayer?
    var onFinish: (() -> Void)?

    /// Returns false when there is no audio for this step.
    @discardableResult
    func play(lessonId: String, step: PreparedStep, muted: Bool) -> Bool {
        stop()
        guard !muted, let url = Self.url(lessonId: lessonId, step: step) else { return false }
        activateSession()
        player = try? AVAudioPlayer(contentsOf: url)
        player?.delegate = self
        player?.rate = 1.0                     // never touched by the speed control
        return player?.play() ?? false
    }

    func stop() { player?.stop(); player = nil }

    private func activateSession() {
        let s = AVAudioSession.sharedInstance()
        try? s.setCategory(.ambient, mode: .spokenAudio,
                           options: [.duckOthers, .interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers])
        try? s.setActive(true)
    }
    func deactivate() {
        try? AVAudioSession.sharedInstance()
              .setActive(false, options: .notifyOthersOnDeactivation)
    }
}

Orientation, landscape and iPad

  • iPhone supports landscape in the player (pl-landscape), and nowhere else in the app. The sheet turns with the phone: in portrait a tall subject draws larger (Palm Tree 317 × 389 pt against 265 × 325 in landscape), in landscape a wide one does (Cat Face 416 pt wide against 358). Which way is better is the subject's shape, so the learner chooses by turning the phone, with no setting and no prompt. §31 says consider landscape “when it materially improves reference + drawing visibility”; with bounds fitting it does, for about half the catalog.
  • UISupportedInterfaceOrientations stays portrait-only for the app; the player's fullScreenCover hosts a UIHostingController subclass that returns .allButUpsideDown from supportedInterfaceOrientations, and calls setNeedsUpdateOfSupportedInterfaceOrientations() on appear and disappear. The rest of the app never rotates.
  • A rotation must not restart the step. The animation is driven by strokeProgress, which is view-model state and survives relayout; DrawingCanvasView recomputes the transform from the new rect each pass, so an in-flight withAnimation(.linear) simply continues at the new size. Do not call replayCurrentStep() from onChange(of: geometry.size). Narration is untouched by rotation.
  • Branch on verticalSizeClass == .compact for the landscape iPhone layout, on measured width for iPad (see pl-ipad), never on UIDevice.current.userInterfaceIdiom.
  • Backgrounding: scenePhase == .background → player.stop() and narration.stop(). Returning shows the step paused mid-draw; resume by replayCurrentStep() on .active only if the phase is still .drawing, so the learner never returns to a frozen half-line.

Open questions

  • Skip ahead while drawing. The outlined primary is enabled so an experienced learner is never held by the animation. The plan's loop is “watch → draw → confirm”; a first-time learner who taps early skips the watch. Alternative: keep it enabled but require a second tap within 2 s (“Tap again to skip”) for the first three lessons only. Recommendation: ship it enabled and plain; the wording “I drew it” is enough of a check.
  • Fit margin. 6 % is drawn here. The Studio's own preview should adopt the same rule so authors see what learners see; decide whether the file records an explicit fit rectangle or the player always computes it.
  • Does every lesson get an orientation beat? Simple House earns one. Lesson 2 (“House With Chimney”) reuses the same idea and would be padding. Proposal: the beat appears only when the tutorial carries a non-empty orientation string, so it is an authoring decision in the Studio, not a runtime one.
  • Silent switch. Decided for this design: .ambient, respected (see the handbook). The counter-argument stands and is worth a creator's call: a learner who props the phone beside the paper with the switch on gets a silent lesson and may not know why; .playback (what Maps does for spoken guidance) would avoid that at the cost of speaking on a silenced phone.
  • Does speed persist? Now that speed lives in the ⋯ menu it reads as a preference; persist it (settings.defaultPlaybackSpeed already exists) rather than resetting per PlayerViewModel.
  • Schema additions needed if you accept the layout: step.detail (the second sentence) and tutorial.orientation / orientationDetail; optionally tutorial.fit. Audio behaviour — the decision

Duck, do not pause — and stay under the silent switch. Session category .ambient with options [.duckOthers, .interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers], activated when a step begins narrating and deactivated with .notifyOthersOnDeactivation when the lesson ends.

  • Why duck: a lesson is a four-minute sequence of 2–3 second clips with long silences while the learner draws. Pausing music would stop and restart it five times in one lesson, which is worse than a short dip. Ducking is also what a navigation app does, and this is the same shape of interruption: short spoken guidance over a continuous background.
  • Spoken audio is different. Ducking a podcast just makes it unintelligible under a voice, so .interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers pauses spoken-audio apps and resumes them on deactivation. Music ducks; podcasts pause.
  • Calls: never interrupt. An AVAudioSession.interruptionNotification with .began stops narration and leaves the animation running — the written instruction is still on screen, so the lesson is not blocked.
  • Silent switch: honoured, and that is what picks the category. .playback deliberately ignores the ring/silent switch, which is right for a music app and wrong here — a learner who silenced their phone in a waiting room did not consent to a voice. .ambient obeys the switch and stops when the screen locks, and both are correct for this app: a locked screen means nobody is watching the step. The row on screen promises exactly this, so do not quietly change the category later.

The voice asset pipeline

  1. The creator writes or edits a step instruction in the Studio.
  2. The Studio calls ElevenLabs with that instruction and the chosen voice, and writes the result to shared/Assets/Voice/<lessonId>/<stepId>.m4a, alongside a voice.json manifest recording the voice id, model, and a hash of the instruction text.
  3. The step's voiceover field (already reserved in tutorial.schema.json v1) carries the bare file name — never a path, for the same reason reference photos are bare names: a relative path can point outside the folder.
  4. A changed instruction changes the hash, and the Studio marks that clip stale and offers to regenerate. Nothing regenerates silently: audio costs money and a re-render of an unchanged line wastes it.
  5. Assets/Voice/ ships as a folder reference in the Xcode project, exactly like Tutorials/, so adding a lesson's audio needs no project change.

The app tolerates a missing file. Bundle.main.url(forResource:) returning nil is not an error: the step plays silently, the narration chip does not appear for that step, and a one-line warning joins PreparedTutorial.warnings (the same array TutorialLoader already fills for clamped durations). Never block a step on audio — the learner has a pen in their hand.

Reduce motion — what “sensible form” means

  • Default state follows UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled; the toggle shown here is an in-app override, so a learner who wants the whole system animated can still calm down this one app. Store as settings.reduceMotionOverride (Bool, default false) and treat the effective value as system || override — the override can only ever reduce motion, never force it back on against a system preference.
  • Reduced form of a step: each stroke appears at full length with a 180 ms cross-fade, in order, honouring the stroke's own duration as the delay before the next one. The learner still sees which line comes next and in what order, which is the entire teaching content; only the travelling pencil tip and the dash-offset sweep are dropped.
  • The pencil-tip dot is removed entirely under reduce motion (it is pure motion) and .stroke.draws collapses to a near-zero duration, which the design system already does at prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Everything non-drawing — screen pushes, the completion reveal, the narration wave — uses .animation(nil) or a cross-fade.

Left-handed layout

  • Mirrors two things only: the persistent reference thumbnail moves from the leading corner of the canvas to the trailing one, and the quiet control row (Back · Replay · Speed) swaps ends with the primary button's alignment.

Three behaviours follow, and all three are already implemented in TutorialLoader:

  • The path screen filters, it does not apologise. Build the lesson list from paths.json, drop every id that did not produce a PreparedTutorial, and number the rows by their position in the filtered list. A gap in the numbers is a bug report the learner cannot act on.
  • Lesson counts (“9 lessons”), progress fractions and path-complete detection all use the filtered list, so a path with a skipped lesson still reaches 100%.
  • The Problems view must not ship visible. Today ContentView shows failureBanner and the full-screen TutorialProblemsView unconditionally. In M7 both move behind #if DEBUG — keep the type, keep the tests, and add an entry point from a debug-only settings row. In release, a failure is written to os_log at .error and nowhere else.
  • Ship-blocking guard: a unit test that loads every file in Tutorials/ and fails if any produces a TutorialLoadFailure that is not isForNewerApp. A lesson silently missing from a release is a content bug that must fail CI, not fail quietly on a learner's phone.

Offline — the decision

There is no offline state in version 1, because there is no online state. The app makes no network request: lessons, catalog, reference photos and voice clips are all bundled. So there is deliberately no reachability check, no spinner, no “no connection” banner, no retry — every one of those would be a lie about how the app works, and each is a surface that can fail.

  • The only honest place to say it is About, as one positive sentence (“No network needed”) rather than an error state. That line is shown here promoted into a card; in the shipping About it sits where the about variant of st-about puts it.
  • Do not link Network.framework and do not add NSAppTransportSecurity exceptions. If a future build needs neither, reviewers and researchers can both verify the claim.
  • This stops being true the day remote downloadable content §39 ships. At that point the first real offline state appears — and it should still be quiet: bundled lessons keep working, and only the “more lessons” surface shows a connection message.
  • One near-miss: the App Store privacy policy link and any mailto: leave the app. Those are the system's problem, not ours, and neither needs a network state inside Paper Coach.

Camera denied

  • Reached from sk-capture when AVCaptureDevice.authorizationStatus(for: .video) is .denied or .restricted. Never present the camera UI first and fail into a black frame.
  • “Open Settings” = UIApplication.shared.open(URL(string: UIApplication.openSettingsURLString)!). It lands on Paper Coach's own settings page, where the Camera switch is.
  • “Choose an existing photo” uses PhotosPicker, which needs no permission at all under PHPickerViewController's out-of-process model — so it is a genuine way out, not a second dead end. Keep it even when the camera is available.
  • Info.plist strings, written as the learner will read them mid-lesson: 
    NSCameraUsageDescription
  "Paper Coach uses the camera to photograph your finished drawing
   so it can go into your sketchbook on this iPhone."

NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription
  "Saves a copy of your finished drawing to your photo library when
   you turn on 'Also save to Photos'."
    NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription (read access) is not needed: PhotosPicker does not require it, and asking for library read access in an app that promises nothing leaves the device is a bad trade.
  • If “Also save to Photos” is on and add-access is denied, save to the sketchbook anyway and show a one-line toast: “Saved to your sketchbook. Photos access is off.” Never lose the photograph.

No lessons — the state that should never ship

  • Triggered when the catalog resolves to zero playable lessons: an empty paths.json, a missing Catalog/ folder, or every tutorial failing to load.
  • Tone: state the fact, remove the blame, offer the one thing that might help. No error code, no “contact support” on a screen the learner cannot act on, and no exclamation mark.
  • The tab bar stays — the Sketchbook and Settings still work, and hiding navigation would make a content bug look like a broken app.
  • Guard rails so it never appears: a release-script check that paths.json is non-empty and every listed lesson resolves; the loader test above; and an assertion in #if DEBUG at launch. This screen exists so that if all three fail, the learner still sees a sentence instead of a blank tab.

Accessibility

  • The reset alert is announced with its message; the destructive role gives VoiceOver the right verb. Do not add a hint that repeats the message.
  • Camera-denied screen: focus moves to the headline on appear (.accessibilityFocused), so a VoiceOver user hears why they are here before the buttons.
  • Empty-curriculum illustration is .accessibilityHidden(true); the headline and body are one readable pair.

Open questions

  • Should “Reset progress” also offer a second, explicit “Delete my sketchbook” inside the same alert? Recommendation: no. Two destructive actions in one dialog is how people lose photographs.
  • Is the debug Problems view worth an in-app entry point in TestFlight builds, not just DEBUG? The creator will be the main TestFlight user, and a bad export found on device is cheaper than one found by a learner.
