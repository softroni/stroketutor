# Sky & Weather: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so ten lessons in a row do not say the same sentence ten
times, and do not sound like ten different teachers either. This is the plan for the ten `sky-weather`
lessons (Starter level, 2026-09-20). The models to follow are the creator's own Strawberry and the
scripts in [fruits-words.md](fruits-words.md), [food-treats-words.md](food-treats-words.md),
[forms-words.md](forms-words.md) and [plants-words.md](plants-words.md).

Sky & Weather is the first path a learner meets, and weather is something that *happens*, so Lina
talks about what the thing does (a cloud floats, a bolt strikes, a wave climbs and tips over) and
the pen follows that movement: a cloud from its flat bottom up, rain from the point of each drop,
a wave from the flat sea up to the hook, a volcano from the crater down.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first.** "Rays are short straight lines." "The crater is the hole at
   the top." A step never opens with only "Start at the top". Then, if it helps, where the pen
   starts and one familiar picture (a rainbow, a letter W, a hook, an X).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no jargon, no sizes. Say "bump" for a cloud's lobes,
   "drop" for rain, "point" for a peak's tip, "snow" or "snowy top" for a cap, "zigzag" for its
   edge, "hook" for the wave's crest, "drip" for a lava stream, "puff" for smoke.
4. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now", "Next", "Then", "One more". About one step in three.
5. **No sentence is shared word for word with another lesson of any path, except a plain color
   step.** Clouds come back three times on this path (cloud, rain-cloud, mountain-storm), drops and
   the bolt twice, mountains twice; each script words them differently; keep those differences.
6. **Color steps name the thing and the color.** Plain is the default; at most one per lesson is
   framed differently.
7. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** Outros are one or
   two short sentences about their drawing, never about getting it right.
8. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the base", "Draw the rays", "Color the cloud". Titles may
   repeat across lessons; they are labels, not speech.
9. **Drawing rules still hold** (see the author-lesson skill): straight edges stay whole; a big
   closed curve is split where a pen would lift (a corner, a dip between two bumps; on a plain
   circle, top and bottom); small closed shapes (a drop, a bubble, a smoke puff, the small bolt in
   the scene) stay whole; one thing is finished before the next; at most six lines in a step; a
   line that hangs from something starts where it joins it.

## Phrases already used by published lessons (do not reuse)

Everything listed under the same heading in [plants-words.md](plants-words.md) and
[forms-words.md](forms-words.md), and every script in the four earlier words documents. In particular:

- Openers: "Here comes a summer favorite …", "Let's cut an orange in half …", "Who wants a donut?",
  "On a hot day …", "Two scoops, please.", "Pizza night.", "Lunch is served.", "A pine tree is easier
  than it looks.", "Think of a beach.", "Think of a party hat.", "Psst.", "Roll the dice."
- Hand-overs: "Watch how it comes together, then it's your turn." (retired), "… then pick up your
  pen.", "… then it's over to you.", "… then you try.", "… then it's your go.", "… then build yours.",
  "… then draw your own.", "… then have a go.", "… then try it.", "… then grow one yourself.",
  "… then start your garden.", "… then plant one on your page.", "Watch it grow, …", "Watch it take
  shape, …".
- Step sentences: "Draw one line along the middle of the leaf. Stop before the tip.", "Start at the
  top. Draw a big letter C down to the bottom.", "Go back to the top. Draw the same curve the other
  way, like a mirror.", "Close the slice with a curved bottom, like a wide smile.", "Slant down to the
  left, down to the right, then join them." (pine-tree: the mountains are worded differently).
- Outros: "That's the Fruits path, finished.", "… that's the Forms path done.", "… that's Food and
  Treats finished.", "Your windowsill is full, and so is the Plants path.", "It looks like you could
  pick it up.", "You can almost hear the waves." (palm-tree: the wave's outro asks about the crash instead).

## The scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep the
voice of these lines and adapt the count. Anything changed is reported back, and all ten are read
side by side before the creator reviews them. `snowflake` and `tornado` follow the first-round pictures the
creator chose to keep.

### sun

(Picture: a yellow disc and eight bold ray lines standing off it. Trace: `--max-outline-width 90`,
or the rays are taken for a colored area; the tracer also shortens thick diagonal lines, so the
rays' center lines were measured from the ink and written into the trace. The circle is split at
the top and the bottom. Each ray runs from the end near the sun outward.)

- intro: "The sun is the easiest thing in the sky to draw: one circle and eight straight rays. Watch the rays go around like a clock, then light up your own page."
- left-half: "The sun is a big circle. From the top, curve around the left side down to the bottom."
- right-half: "Now close the circle. From the top again, curve around the right side."
- rays-up-down: "Rays are short straight lines. Draw one above the sun and one below, not touching it."
- rays-sides: "Next, one ray on the left and one on the right."
- rays-corners: "Four more rays fit in the gaps, slanting out like an X."
- sun: "Color the sun yellow."
- outro: "Eight rays and a big yellow middle. Your page just got warmer."

### cloud

(Picture: flat base, rounded left corner with a tiny dip above it, then a small, a tall, a medium
and an end bump. Trace: one closed line. Cut it at the two ends of the base and at every dip.)

- intro: "Lie on the grass, look up, and there's a cloud drifting by. Ours is flat underneath and bumpy on top. See the bumps roll along, then float one of your own."
- base: "A cloud sits on a flat bottom. Draw one straight line from left to right."
- corner: "Round off the left end. From the line, curl out and up to a little dip."
- small-bump: "Now the bumps. The first is a small hill, up and over to the next dip."
- big-bump: "The second bump is the biggest. Go high, like a rainbow, and come down."
- middle-bump: "Then a medium bump, a little lower."
- last-bump: "The last bump curls around and lands on the right end of the line."
- cloud: "Color the cloud gray."
- outro: "There it floats, flat below and puffy on top. What shape do you see in your cloud?"

### lightning-bolt

(Picture: seven straight sides, one needle-sharp point. Trace: one closed line and a short tail at
the point, where the two outlines run together. Cut the closed line at its seven corners and at the
tail's junction; join the tail to the long right side so that side ends at the very tip; the last
left side ends where it meets that line.)

- intro: "Flash. Lightning is over in a blink, but yours will stay on the page. It's a zigzag of seven straight lines. Follow it down once, then strike."
- top: "The bolt begins with its flat top. Draw a short line from left to right."
- zig: "Now zig. From the right end, slant down to the left, then step out to the right."
- zag: "And zag. One long slant, down to a sharp point."
- left-zig: "Back to the top left corner. Slant down, then step in to the right."
- left-zag: "Last line. Slant down until you meet the point."
- bolt: "Color the bolt yellow."
- outro: "Sharp at the bottom and bright all over. Did you hear the thunder?"

### rain-cloud

(Picture: a cloud with four bumps counting its two round ends, five teardrops in rows of three and
two. Trace: six closed lines. Cut the cloud at the ends of its base and at its three dips; the
drops stay whole, each begun at its point.)

- intro: "Uh-oh, that cloud is full. Five raindrops are already on their way down, blown sideways by the wind. Watch them fall in two rows, then make it rain."
- base: "Begin underneath the cloud. One long flat line, left to right."
- left-bumps: "Puff up the left half. A small bump first, then a tall one."
- right-bumps: "Two more puffs bring the cloud back down to the line."
- top-row: "A raindrop is a slanted teardrop, pointed on top and round below. Draw three in a row."
- bottom-row: "Two more drops fall lower, under the gaps."
- cloud: "Color the cloud gray."
- drops: "The raindrops are blue."
- outro: "Five drops, all slanting the same way. Good thing paper doesn't get wet."

### snowflake

(Picture: the creator's choice, kept from the first round: six outlined light-blue arms, each with two
branches near its tip, a round center, six small blue-green diamonds between the arms. The ink
trace rounds the notches into blobs, so the lines are the color edges (`svg trace
--ink-max-channel 0`) moved out by half the ink's width to its center line: six closed arms, one
circle, six diamonds, all checked on the ink. Each arm is cut into five lines: up one side, the
first branch out and back, over the tip, the second branch, down the other side; the short piece
lying on the circle's line goes. One arm per step, clockwise from the top. The colors are not in
the palette and are left as the picture has them. 43 lines: expect a long-lesson warning.)

- intro: "No two snowflakes are alike, but every one has six arms. Ours has a round middle, six branching arms and six tiny diamonds. Watch one arm closely, the rest are copies, then let it snow."
- center: "A snowflake grows from its middle. Draw a small circle in the center of your page."
- arm-top: "The first arm points up. Up one side, out and back for a branch, over the tip, then down the same way."
- arm-upper-right: "The next arm leans to the upper right. It is the same shape, just turned."
- arm-lower-right: "Now the lower right arm. Side, branch, tip, branch, side."
- arm-bottom: "The fourth arm points straight down, like the first one upside down."
- arm-lower-left: "Two arms to go. This one reaches to the lower left."
- arm-upper-left: "The last arm fills the gap on the upper left. Now there are six."
- diamonds: "Between the arms sit six little diamonds. Each is a tiny kite with soft corners."
- arms-color: "Color the arms and the center light blue."
- diamonds-color: "Color the diamonds blue-green."
- outro: "Six arms, six diamonds and one frosty middle. This snowflake will never melt."

### wave

(Picture: one blue body with a low swell on the left, a hooked crest with a cream foam cap of
many small bumps, two flow lines, and three spray bubbles the prompt did not ask for; they are kept
as an easy step. The white shine dots in the bubbles are not drawn. Trace: eight lines; the long
outline is cut at the ends of the base, at the dip after the swell, where the foam begins and at
the tip of the hook. The foam's bumpy edge may be cut in two at a dip.)

- intro: "Surf's up. A wave rises, curls over and crashes into foam. We'll catch it just before the crash. Watch the water climb, then ride your own."
- base: "The sea is flat underneath. Draw a long straight line from left to right."
- swell: "Behind every big wave comes a small one. From the left end, draw a low hump."
- back: "Now the water climbs. Sweep up and to the right in one long curve."
- crest: "At the top the wave tips over. Curve forward and down into a hook."
- hollow: "Under the hook it's hollow. Scoop around and down to the flat line."
- foam: "Foam rides on top. Draw a row of small bumps from the back of the wave to the hook."
- flow-lines: "Two curved lines inside show the water rushing up."
- bubbles: "Spray flies off the top. Three bubbles, just small circles."
- water: "Color the water blue, and the bubbles too."
- foam-color: "Color the foam cream."
- outro: "There it goes, curling over with foam flying. Can you hear it crash?"

### mountains

(Picture: a green front peak, a brown one behind on the left, a blue one behind on the right, a
cream zigzag cap on each, one shared ground line. Trace: five lines, one of them very long; cut it
at every point, corner and junction so each slope and each stretch of ground is its own line. A
back peak starts on the front peak's slope, rises to its point and comes down the far side.)

- intro: "Far away, three mountains stand in a row, so high that their tops stay snowy all year. The big one stands in front. Notice which lines stop behind it, then climb your own."
- front: "The big mountain goes first. From its point, slant down each way, then draw the ground between."
- front-snow: "Snow covers the top. Zigzag across from slope to slope, like a letter W."
- left: "A second mountain peeks out on the left. Go up from the big slope to a point, down, and back along the ground."
- left-snow: "It has snow too. Draw a smaller zigzag near its point."
- right: "One more on the right, the smallest. Up from the slope, down the far side, flat underneath."
- right-snow: "Give the last top its snow, one more little zigzag."
- front-color: "Color the big mountain green."
- left-color: "Color the left mountain brown."
- right-color: "The right mountain is blue."
- snow: "Color all three snowy tops cream."
- outro: "Three mountains, and the big one clearly in front. That trick is called overlap, and now it's yours."

### tornado

(Picture: the creator's choice, kept from the first round: a long oval rim with a deeper blue inside,
a light blue bowl under it, then a purple band, a light blue band, a small purple band and a light
blue comma tail, with three short motion marks beside it. The bands are open at the top, and the
color runs a little past the ends of their lines, as in the picture. The marks are gray in the
picture and are traced as ordinary lines (`svg trace --ink-max-channel 150`). The two blues are kept
apart by converting without snapping to the palette. The long traced line is cut so the rim, the
bowl and the first band are each their own lines.)

- intro: "A tornado is wind spinning so fast that it turns into a funnel. Ours is a wide bowl on top, three shrinking bands and a curly tail. Watch it twist, then spin your own."
- rim: "The top of the funnel is a long flat oval. Draw the upper curve, then the lower one."
- bowl: "Under the oval hangs a big bowl. Curve down from the left end, then down from the right and across."
- band-two: "The next band tucks in below, shaped like a wide smile."
- band-three: "Then a smaller smile under that one."
- band-four: "One more, the smallest."
- tail: "The tail curls down to a point, like a comma."
- marks: "Three short curved marks beside the funnel show it spinning."
- inside-color: "Color the inside of the oval blue."
- light-blue: "Color the bowl, the third band and the tail light blue."
- purple: "Color the other two bands purple."
- outro: "Wide on top, a curly tail below, and it really looks like it's spinning. Hold on to your hat."

### volcano

(Picture: a red crater ellipse, bumpy brown slopes, three orange lava drips of which the left one
spills over the shoulder, three clover-shaped gray puffs growing as they rise. Trace: seven lines;
the cone is cut at the two ends of the crater and the two feet; the puffs stay whole. Adapt the
lava steps to how the trace gives the drips.)

- intro: "Rumble, rumble. This volcano is awake. Lava spills down its sides and smoke puffs out of the top. We begin up at the crater. Watch it erupt, then set off your own."
- crater: "The crater is the hole at the top. Draw a flat oval, the far edge first, then the near edge."
- left-slope: "Now the mountain. From the left of the crater, wobble down and out to the ground."
- right-slope: "The right side comes down the same way. Bumpy is fine. It's rock."
- ground: "Join the two feet with a straight line."
- big-drip: "Lava pours over the rim. Draw a long drip down the middle with a round end."
- small-drips: "Shorter drips run down on either side. The left one spills over the edge."
- smoke: "Smoke rises in three puffs: small, bigger, biggest, drifting to the right."
- mountain: "Color the mountain brown."
- lava: "Color the lava orange."
- crater-color: "Inside the crater it's red hot. Color it red."
- smoke-color: "Color the smoke gray."
- outro: "Lava, smoke and a mountain of rock. Luckily this volcano only erupts on paper."

### mountain-storm

(Picture: a gray cloud, a small yellow bolt, four blue drops, a green peak in front and a light
blue one behind on the right, jagged cream caps. Trace: ten lines, the peaks' outline very long;
an 18-unit tail at the bolt's point is a tracing artifact and goes. The light blue of the far peak
is left off-palette so it stays a separate area from the drops. Twelve steps at most: the cloud is
one step, both caps share a step.)

- intro: "Everything from this path meets in one picture: a cloud, a lightning bolt, rain and two snowy mountains. See where each piece goes, then bring on the storm."
- cloud: "High up goes the storm cloud. Flat line first, then the bumps from left to right."
- bolt: "Under the middle of the cloud, draw a small zigzag bolt with a sharp point."
- drops: "Rain falls on both sides. Two slanted drops on the left, two on the right."
- near-peak: "Down below stands the near mountain. Two slopes from one point, and the ground under them."
- far-peak: "Behind it on the right, a smaller mountain. Its left side stops at the near one."
- snow: "Both tops get a jagged line of snow."
- cloud-color: "Color the cloud gray."
- bolt-color: "Color the bolt yellow."
- drops-color: "Color the raindrops blue."
- near-color: "The near mountain is green."
- far-color: "Make the far mountain light blue."
- snow-color: "Color the snow cream."
- outro: "Thunder, rain and snowy mountains, all in one picture. That's a whole storm, and the whole Sky and Weather path."
