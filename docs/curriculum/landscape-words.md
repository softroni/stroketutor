# Landscape: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so ten lessons in a row do not say the same sentence ten
times, and do not sound like ten different teachers either. This is the plan for the `landscape`
lessons (Advanced level, 2026-09-21), written from the pictures as they came back, not from the
prompts. The models to follow are the creator's own Strawberry and the scripts in
[fruits-words.md](fruits-words.md), [food-treats-words.md](food-treats-words.md),
[forms-words.md](forms-words.md), [plants-words.md](plants-words.md),
[sky-weather-words.md](sky-weather-words.md), [wheels-words.md](wheels-words.md),
[on-the-water-words.md](on-the-water-words.md), [in-the-air-words.md](in-the-air-words.md) and
[space-words.md](space-words.md).

Landscape is the path about distance, so Lina talks like someone looking out at a view: the thing
that is far away is smaller, paler, higher up the page, or hidden behind the thing in front. Each
lesson has one of those tricks, and the intro names it in plain words, never as a rule. A child who
has done three of these should expect the far thing to be drawn behind the near one, and to hear
why, without hearing the same sentence twice.

**Where a picture differs from its prompt, the words follow the picture.** The creator changed
several: `rolling-hills` has two pine trees and no horizon line; `mountain-range` has snow caps,
two green hills and four trees; `winding-river` has a sun and a band of low hills; `lake-reflection`
has bushes along the shore; `waterfall` has four bushes, a lumpy cliff and a ripple each side of
every rock; `sea-cliffs` has grass on both headlands and no outline down the right of the sea;
`forest-path` has five bushes; `country-road` has a sun with eight rays, bushes and two trees;
`mountain-valley` has no side edges on the valley floor. The vanishing-point dot at the top of the
road in `country-road` was painted out of the picture at the creator's request, so the road's two
edges simply meet the far line.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first.** "The lake hangs below it, like a bowl." "Five posts down the
   right side." A step never opens with only "Start at the left". Then, if it helps, where the pen
   starts and one familiar picture (a bowl, a zigzag, a cloud, a wide arch, a step).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no jargon, no sizes. Landscape words are kept to a few
   and used the same way everywhere: "the ground" or "the sand" for the line everything stands on,
   "the far line" for the level line where flat land ends, "behind" and "in front" for depth, "bank"
   for the side of a river. Never "horizon", "vanishing point", "perspective", "foreground" or
   "background": the picture teaches those, the words do not name them.
4. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now", "Next", "Then", "Last". About one step in three.
5. **No sentence is shared word for word with another lesson of any path, except a plain color
   step.** Hills, banks, level lines and pointed trees come back on nearly every lesson here, so each
   script words them differently; keep those differences.
6. **Color steps name the thing and the color.** `svg from-image` gives one area per color, so a
   lesson has one color step per color, or two small colors together; the step names every part that
   color covers, largest first. Plain is the default; at most one per lesson is framed differently.
   Lina says "green" for leaf green, "dark green", "pale green", "cream", "gray".
7. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** Outros are one or
   two short sentences about their drawing, never about getting it right.
8. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the far hill", "Color the lake". Titles may repeat across
   lessons; they are labels, not speech.
9. **Drawing rules still hold** (see the author-lesson skill). On this path that means: the line
   everything stands on is drawn first, left to right; a shape that is behind another is drawn after
   the ground and before the shape in front of it, and its line stops where it meets what is already
   on the page; a big closed outline is cut where a pen would lift (a cliff at its top corners, a
   pine at its tip); small closed shapes (a sun, a rock, a post, a bush, a cabin roof) stay whole; a
   tree is finished before the next tree; at most six lines in a step; a river or a road is drawn
   from the near end (wide, at the bottom) toward the far end.

## The scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep the
voice of these lines and adapt the count. Anything changed is reported back, and all ten are read
side by side before any voice is recorded. Colors are listed in the order they are painted, largest
area first.

### rolling-hills

Three hills that overlap, two pine trees, a sun. No horizon line in the picture.

- intro: "Three hills, one behind another. The one in front hides the feet of the others, and that is what makes a flat page look deep. Watch them line up, then let your own hills roll."
- ground: "The ground is one straight line. Draw it left to right along the bottom."
- back-hill: "The hill at the back is a wide arch. Leave a gap at its top for a trunk."
- second-hill: "The next hill arches up on the right, back down to the ground."
- front-hill: "One long arch across the front, corner to corner. It cuts over both hills."
- left-tree: "A pine stands in the gap. Short trunk first, then the spiky shape above it."
- right-tree: "The other pine is smaller, over on the second hill. Trunk, then spikes."
- sun: "Last, a plain circle high in the sky, clear of everything."
- colors: "Color the front hill dark green." / "The hill on the right and both pines are green." / "The far hill on the left is pale green." / "The sun is yellow and the two trunks are brown."
- outro: "Three hills, and anyone can tell which one is nearest. That is depth, on a flat page."

### desert-dunes

Three dunes, each with a crest line and a shadow side; two cacti, the far one much smaller.

- intro: "Out in the desert there is nothing to measure by except a cactus. Ours is drawn twice, once big and once tiny, and that tiny one is what puts the far dune miles away. See it happen, then it's your turn in the sand."
- sand: "Flat sand first, one long line from left to right."
- back-dune-left: "The tall dune humps up from the left end and comes down again."
- back-dune-right: "A rounder one humps up on the right, with sand showing between them."
- front-dune: "The wide dune in front runs across both, low and slow."
- back-crests: "Each dune behind has a line from its top down to its edge. Draw both."
- front-crest: "The front dune gets one too, curving down to the right."
- near-cactus: "The big cactus is one outline. Up the post, then out and up for each arm."
- far-cactus: "The same shape again, much smaller, sitting on the dune behind."
- colors: "Color the sunny side of each dune yellow." / "The strip past each crest line is orange." / "Both cacti are dark green."
- outro: "Your desert runs a long way back. The little cactus does all the work."

### mountain-range

Three peaks, snow on the outer two, a green hill behind and one in front, four pines.

- intro: "Mountains lose their color in the distance. These peaks are blue, the snow is gray and the hills in front are the darkest green on the page. Watch the layers pile up, then send your own peaks back."
- ground: "Everything stands on one level line. Draw it near the bottom."
- peaks: "Three peaks in a row. Up and down for the left one, then the middle, then the right."
- snow: "Snow caps the outer two. Draw a wobbly line under each cap."
- back-hill: "A low green hill rises on the right and runs into the mountain."
- front-hill: "The front hill sweeps up from the left corner, over to the right."
- left-trees: "Two pines on the left hill. Trunk and spiky top for each."
- right-trees: "Two more on the right, the same way."
- colors: "Color the mountain blue." / "The front hill and all four pines are dark green." / "The hill behind is green." / "The snow caps are gray." / "The trunks are brown."
- outro: "Hold your page at arm's length. The pale peaks really do sit at the back."

### winding-river

An S-shaped river narrowing to a point, two fields, a band of hills, a sun.

- intro: "A river is the same width all the way along, but on paper it has to get thinner as it goes, or it will never reach the hills. Watch the two banks close in, then let your river run."
- far-line: "One long line across the page is where the fields end."
- hills: "Low hills sit on that line. One bumpy line over the top of them."
- left-bank-near: "The river is widest at the bottom. Draw its left bank up to the first bend."
- left-bank-far: "Carry on from the bend, getting closer to the middle all the way."
- right-bank-near: "Now the right bank, from the bottom up to its own bend."
- right-bank-far: "Follow it up. The two banks meet in a point on the line."
- sun: "One circle in the sky, well above the water."
- colors: "Color the fields pale green." / "The river is blue." / "The hills are green." / "The sun is yellow."
- outro: "Your river gets thinner all the way to the hills. That is how you send water into the distance."

### lake-reflection

A purple peak and three pines on the shore, each mirrored in the lake; bushes; four ripples.

- intro: "Still water copies whatever stands beside it, only upside down and paler. We will draw everything twice. Watch the lake fill up, then fill your own."
- shore: "The far shore is one straight line across the middle."
- lake: "The lake hangs below it. One big curve from end to end, like a bowl."
- peak: "A mountain stands on the shore. Up one slope to the point, then down the other."
- pines: "Three pointed trees stand to the right of it, each a narrow triangle."
- reflections: "Now the water copies them. Draw each shape again below the line, upside down."
- bushes: "Soft bushes fill the shore between the trees, in low bumps."
- ripples: "Four short flat lines float in the water, spread well apart."
- colors: "Color the lake blue." / "The mountain is purple and its reflection is gray." / "The three trees are dark green." / "Their reflections are pale green." / "The bushes are green."
- outro: "Two drawings for the price of one: the real one, and the one in the water."

### waterfall

A lumpy brown cliff split by falling water, three water lines, a mist puff, a pool with three rocks
and six ripples, four bushes.

- intro: "Falling water has no shape of its own, so we show it with straight lines and a puff of mist where it lands. Watch it come down, then turn your own water on."
- cliff: "The cliff is a tall block with lumpy sides. A gap runs down the middle."
- fall: "Two straight lines drop through the gap, from top to bottom."
- water-lines: "Three more straight lines inside, side by side, the middle one lower."
- mist: "Where the water lands there is mist. Draw a cloud with three bumps on top."
- pool: "The pool spreads out below in one wide, wavy curve."
- rocks: "Three rocks sit in the water, each a low dome, all different sizes."
- ripples: "A small curve each side of every rock, like water moving around it."
- bushes: "Bushes grow on the cliff, four soft clumps at the corners."
- colors: "Color the falling water and the pool blue." / "The cliff and the three rocks are brown." / "The bushes are green." / "The mist is gray."
- outro: "You can almost feel the spray, and it is only straight lines and one cloud."

### sea-cliffs

Two headlands, the far one lower and cream, a gray sea stack crossing the far line, three waves.

- intro: "Two cliffs reach out into the sea, one behind the other, and a lone rock stands out in the water. Watch the second cliff stop where the first one gets in its way, then walk your own coast."
- near-top: "The near cliff is a tall block. Draw its left edge and its flat top."
- near-face: "From the top corner, down the cliff face and along the foot."
- far-cliff: "A second cliff steps down behind it, lower and further right."
- grass: "Grass caps both cliffs. A wavy line under each top edge."
- far-line: "The sea meets the sky in one level line, in two pieces."
- stack: "One tall rock stands alone in the water, its top above that line."
- waves: "Three wavy lines cross the sea, the lowest one longest."
- colors: "Color the near cliff brown." / "The sea is blue." / "The cliff behind is cream." / "The grassy tops are green." / "The lone rock is gray."
- outro: "One cliff in front, one behind, and a rock out on its own. Your coast is finished."

### forest-path

One canopy, six trunks in three sizes, a cream path, five bushes.

- intro: "Walk into a forest and the trees beside you are huge, while the ones ahead are little. Ours hang from one green cloud, and they shrink as the path goes in. Watch the forest open up, then open your own path."
- canopy: "The leaves are one long cloud across the top, with round humps."
- front-trunks: "Two thick trunks hang from it, one at each side. Both reach the ground."
- middle-trunks: "Two thinner trunks stand inside them, stopping higher up."
- back-trunks: "Two more, thinner still, nearest the middle of the page."
- path-left: "The path is wide at the bottom. Draw its left edge, curving inward."
- path-right: "Its right edge does the same, and the two nearly meet far away."
- left-bushes: "Round bushes sit at the feet of the trees on the left."
- right-bushes: "Two more bushes on the right, the same soft bumps."
- colors: "Color the leaves dark green." / "The trunks are brown." / "The path is cream." / "The bushes are green."
- outro: "The path really goes somewhere. Your trees get smaller, so the forest keeps going."

### country-road

A land panel, a cream road running to one point, five shrinking posts, bushes, two trees, a sun with
eight rays. The dot where the road's edges meet has been taken out of the picture.

- intro: "Here is the oldest trick there is: two edges of a road, aimed at one spot far away. Everything beside it shrinks as it goes. Watch the posts get smaller, then follow your own road."
- panel: "The land is one wide box. Draw the bottom edge and both sides."
- far-line: "A level line across the top of the box. That is as far as you can see."
- road: "The road is a triangle. Two lines from the bottom, meeting at one spot on that line."
- posts: "Five posts down the right side. The nearest is tallest, each one smaller."
- hedges: "Bushes line the far edge in low bumps. Two tall trees stand on the right."
- sun: "A circle up in the sky. One straight ray above, below and each side."
- rays: "Four more rays go in the gaps between them. Eight in all."
- colors: "Color the road cream." / "The left field is green." / "The right field is pale green." / "The bushes are dark green." / "The posts and trunks are brown, and the sun is yellow."
- outro: "Five posts, five sizes, one road. Your page suddenly has a long way to go."

### mountain-valley

The whole path in one picture: a big pine in front, cabin and river in the middle, two pale peaks
behind.

- intro: "This one has all of it: something huge in front, something small in the middle, and pale mountains a long way behind. Take it slowly, tree last. Watch the valley come together, then put your own one together."
- lines: "Two level lines: one along the bottom, one where the grass ends."
- peaks: "Two mountains sit on the far line, two straight slopes each."
- river: "The river runs from the bottom up to where the mountains meet. Both banks."
- cabin: "A small cabin on the right: square wall, pointed roof, one door."
- pine-left: "The big tree in front is a zigzag. From the tip, step down its left side."
- pine-right: "Back to the tip and down the right side, the same steps."
- trunk: "A short trunk under it, standing on the grass."
- colors: "Color the grass pale green." / "The mountains are gray." / "The river is blue." / "The tree is dark green." / "The trunk and the cabin wall are brown. The roof is red, the door cream."
- outro: "Front, middle and far away, all in one valley. That is every trick on this path in one drawing."
