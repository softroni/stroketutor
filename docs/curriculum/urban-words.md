# Urban: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so twelve lessons in a row do not say the same sentence
twelve times, and do not sound like twelve different teachers either. This is the plan for the
`urban` lessons (Advanced level, learners 16 and up, 2026-09-29), written from the
pictures as they come back, not from the prompts in
[urban-prompts.md](urban-prompts.md). A lesson's script is added here when its
picture is kept. The models to follow are [landscape-words.md](landscape-words.md) (the last
Advanced path) and the voice of [plants-words.md](plants-words.md) and
[food-treats-words.md](food-treats-words.md), which the creator likes best.

Urban is the path about drawing a town as it stands, so Lina talks like someone on a
bench with a sketchbook: a building is a few boxes, a row of the same thing is counted, a brick
wall or a cobbled street is a few marks that stand for the rest, and the thing in front hides the
thing behind. The learners are grown-ups who never learned to draw, so she is warm and plain, a
little less playful than on the children's paths, never cute and never technical.

**Where a picture differs from its prompt, the words follow the picture.** The creator changed
`shop-front`: a pale green tree was added behind the shop's left side. `balcony`: the flower box's
shadow was taken out, and the railing has six bars, the doors' sides standing in for two more.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first.** "The awning hangs under the sign." "Five books stand on the
   bottom of the glass." A step never opens with only "Start at the left". Then, if it helps, where
   the pen starts and one familiar picture (a box, a lid, a bump, a stripe).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no sizes. Plain words for the parts of a building:
   "the top" or "the lid" (not cornice), "the ledge under the window" (not sill), "the wedge at the
   top of the arch" (not keystone), "the awning", "the sign", "the panel". Never "perspective",
   "vanishing point", "eye level", "horizon", "two-point" or "foreground": the pictures teach
   those, the words do not name them. Say instead "the far end is shorter", "these lines lean
   toward the same far point", "the level line at the height of your eyes".
4. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now", "Next", "Then", "Last". About one step in three.
5. **No sentence is shared word for word with another lesson of any path, except a plain color
   step.** Boxes, windows and doors come back in nearly every lesson here, so each script words
   them differently; keep those differences.
6. **Color steps name the thing and the color.** `svg from-image` gives one area per color, and
   these pictures have many colors, so a color step may carry two or three colors that belong
   together (the awning's red and cream, the frames' yellow and the glass's blue), naming every
   part each color covers, largest first. Lina says "green" for leaf green, "dark green", "pale
   green", "cream", "gray".
7. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** About eight to
   ten seconds. Outros are one or two short sentences about their drawing, never about getting it
   right, and end with a question or a light joke.
8. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the wall", "Color the awning". Titles may repeat.
9. **Drawing rules still hold** (see the author-lesson skill): the biggest box first, then what sits
   on it; each new line starts on something already drawn; a thing behind is drawn after the thing
   in front of it, and its line stops where it meets it (the tree behind the shop is drawn last,
   from the wall back to the wall); a long curvy outline is cut where a pen would lift; small
   closed shapes stay whole; at most six lines in a step.

Everything listed under the same heading in [landscape-words.md](landscape-words.md), and every

## The scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep
the voice of these lines and adapt. Colors are listed in the order they are painted, largest area
first.

### shop-front

A green bookshop: a flat top, a framed blank sign, a seven-stripe awning, a framed window with two
shine lines and five books pressed together, two panels, a door with glass, shine, a panel and a
knob; outside on the left a potted bush, and a pale green tree behind the shop's left side.

- intro: "Sketchers see a street as a stack of boxes. This bookshop is a wall, a sign, a window and a door, plus a few extras. Watch it open, then open a shop of your own."
- wall: "The shop starts as one big box. Draw its four sides."
- top: "A long flat lid sits on top, a little wider than the box."
- sign: "Near the top, a long sign with a smaller box inside it. Both stay blank."
- awning: "The awning hangs under the sign. Level on top, sides leaning out, seven bumps below."
- stripes: "Six lines split it into seven stripes. Each one ends where two bumps meet."
- window: "The shop window is a box inside a box. Two short slanted lines make the glass shine."
- books: "Five books stand on the bottom of the glass. The tallest is in the middle."
- panels: "Under the window, two long panels side by side."
- door: "Next, the door, standing on the ground. Glass on top, a panel below, a round knob."
- pot-bush: "A pot stands outside on the left, narrow at the bottom. A bumpy bush sits on it."
- tree: "Last, a tree behind the shop. Its bumps start on the wall and end on the wall."
- colors: "Color the wall dark green." / "Green for the lid, the panels, the bush and one book. The tree is pale green." / "Red and cream for the stripes, and a book of each. The inside of the sign is cream." / "Yellow for the two frames and the knob. Both panes of glass are blue." / "The door is brown. Orange for its panel, the pot and a book. The tall book is purple."
- outro: "Your shop is open for business. What would you put in the window?"

### balcony

A yellow wall with a pair of green doors under a pointed hood: a band with a low triangle on it, a
pane with two shine lines in each door and two round knobs. The doors stand on a cream slab held up
by two orange brackets; a railing of one level rail and six bars stands in front of them (the doors'
sides run down behind it and look like two more bars). On the right a flower box with a bumpy bush
and three red flowers; up the left a climbing plant with a winding stem, two red flowers and four
leaves. No shadow: it was taken out of the picture. The railing is drawn before the doors: it stands
in front, and the doors' outline starts and ends on it; the doors' lower edges below the rail are
two of its bars (2026-09-30, after the creator saw a jog in the doors' right side). The bush's three
flowers have no outline, so they are only colored in, as dots, in the last step.

- intro: "Look up in any old town and you'll spot balconies. This one has green doors, a pointed hood and a rail to lean on. Watch it come out, then step onto your own."
- wall: "The wall comes first: one big box, a little taller than wide."
- slab: "Low down, a long stone slab runs almost from side to side."
- brackets: "Two brackets hold it up. Curved on the outside, straight on the inside."
- rail: "Next, the railing, which stands in front. One level rail, with a bar down from each end."
- bars: "Six more bars between them, spaced out evenly."
- doors: "The doors stand behind the rail: up from it, across, and back down. Add a line down the middle."
- hood: "Over the doors, a flat band with a low pointed roof on top."
- glass: "A tall pane in each door, each with two short slanted lines."
- knobs: "Two round knobs just below the glass, one each side of the middle."
- flower-box: "On the right, a box a little wider at the top. A bush of five bumps sits in it."
- vine-stem: "Up the left side, a winding stem with a round flower halfway and one at the top."
- leaves: "Four leaves grow off the stem, two on each side."
- colors: "Color the wall yellow, and the two knobs as well." / "Green for the doors, the bush and the four leaves." / "Cream for the slab and the hood. The glass is blue." / "Orange for the brackets and the box. Red for the two flowers, and three dots on the bush."
- outro: "Your balcony is ready. Who would you wave to from up there?"

### phone-box

A red phone box seen from its corner: a red left side and a brown right side (the shade side), a
cream and a yellow sign band along their tops, a red domed roof with a small yellow knob, six blue
panes on each side, a door handle low on the left side, a gray base turning the corner, and a pale
green tree behind the right side.

- intro: "A red phone box is the easiest landmark in town. We see it from its corner, so both sides lean away from us. Watch them lean, then stand one on your own street."
- corner: "Begin with the near corner: one tall upright line."
- sides: "Two sides go back from it. Their tops slope down and their bottoms slope up."
- bands: "A band along the top of each side, sloping the same way."
- roof: "The roof is one round curve from far corner to far corner, with a small knob on top."
- left-panes: "Six panes on the left side, three rows of two, tilted like the side."
- right-panes: "Six more on the right. The far ones are a little narrower."
- base: "Along the bottom, a low band turns the corner. A short line on the left is the handle."
- tree: "A round, bumpy tree peeks out from behind the right side."
- colors: "Color the left side red, and the roof too." / "The right side is brown, because it's in the shade." / "Cream and yellow for the two bands. The knob is yellow." / "All twelve panes are blue." / "Gray for the base, and pale green for the tree."
- outro: "Your phone box is ready for a call. Who would you ring first?"

### street-corner

A corner café building in two-point view: a short level line on each side at the height of the
door (the eye line), a yellow left wall and an orange right wall meeting at the near corner, a
cream band along the top turning the corner, a chimney (red front, brown side) on it, a brown door
with a blue pane on the left wall, a big blue café window with two shine lines on the right wall
under a red and cream awning of five scallops, eight blue windows on the two upper floors, six
cream ledges, and two brown flower boxes with green bushes under the first-floor windows (on the
left wall under the far window, on the right wall under the near one). The flowers in the bushes
are tiny red dots with no outline, colored in the last step.

- intro: "Stand on any street corner and look up: the walls run away from you on both sides. This one has a little café downstairs. Watch it come together, then build one on your own block."
- eye-line: "Start with two short level lines, one on each side, at the height of your eyes."
- corner: "Next, the near corner: one tall upright line between them."
- walls: "Each wall runs back from the corner, its top tipping down and its foot tipping up."
- top-band: "Along the roofline, a cream band that turns the corner."
- chimney: "A small chimney sits on the band, two faces and a flat top."
- door: "On the left wall, the door stands on the ground, with a window in it."
- cafe-window: "On the right, the big café window, with two short lines for shine."
- awning: "Over it, the awning: five bumps along the bottom, four lines between the stripes."
- left-windows: "Four windows up the left wall, two on each floor, leaning like the wall."
- right-windows: "Four more on the right wall. The far ones come out a little smaller."
- ledges: "A ledge under six of the windows, tilted like each window."
- flower-boxes: "The last two windows get a flower box instead, each with a bumpy bush on top."
- colors: "Color the left wall yellow and the right wall orange." / "Cream for the top band, the ledges and two stripes." / "Every window is blue, the café window and the door's pane too." / "Brown for the door, the flower boxes and one side of the chimney." / "Red for the chimney's front, three stripes and the tiny flowers. Green for both bushes."
- outro: "Your corner café is open. What's the first thing you'd order?"
