# Plants: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so ten lessons in a row do not say the same sentence ten
times, and do not sound like ten different teachers either. This is the plan for the ten new `plants`
lessons (Core level, 2026-09-20). The models to follow are the creator's own Strawberry and the scripts
in [fruits-words.md](fruits-words.md), [food-treats-words.md](food-treats-words.md) and
[forms-words.md](forms-words.md). `palm-tree-4` is published and stays as it is; its words are long
adult sentences ("Start at the ground and draw the two long sides of the trunk upward to the crown"),
and nothing here borrows from them.

Plants is the path where things grow, so Lina draws the way a plant grows: from the ground up, the
trunk or stem before what it carries, and each leaf from where it joins the stem out to its tip.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first.** "The tip of the pine is a small triangle." "Gills are short
   lines under the cap." A step never opens with only "Start at the top". Then, if it helps, where
   the pen starts and one familiar picture (a rainbow, a letter L, a thumb, a cloud).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no jargon, no sizes. Say "the post" or "the body" for
   the cactus column, "treetop" or "the green" for a canopy, "layer" for a bonsai pad, "dashes" for
   spines, "rim" for the band on a pot.
4. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now", "Next", "Then", "One more". About one step in three.
5. **No sentence is shared word for word with another lesson of any path, except a plain color
   step.** Stems, leaves and center veins come back four times on this path (tulip, sunflower, rose,
   windowsill), so each script words them differently; keep those differences.
6. **Color steps name the thing and the color.** Plain is the default; at most one per lesson is
   framed differently. Lina says "light green", "green", "dark green", "pink" for watermelon pink.
7. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** Outros are one or
   two short sentences about their drawing, never about getting it right.
8. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the trunk", "Draw the left arm", "Color the cap".
   Titles may repeat across lessons; they are labels, not speech.
9. **Drawing rules still hold** (see the author-lesson skill): straight edges stay whole; a big
   closed curve is split where a pen would lift (a tip, a corner, a dip between two bumps); small
   closed shapes (a spot, a pot rim) stay whole; one thing is finished before the next; at most six
   lines in a step; a line that grows from something starts where it joins it.

## Phrases already used by published lessons (do not reuse)

Everything listed under the same heading in [forms-words.md](forms-words.md), and the Forms scripts
themselves. In particular:

- Openers: "Every box you'll ever draw starts here …", "A cylinder is a tin can …", "Think of a party
  hat.", "A circle is flat.", "Roll the dice.", "Somebody is getting a present.", "Psst.", "A mug of
  hot chocolate …", "A book is a flat box …", "Artists call a group of objects a still life."
- Hand-overs: "… then draw your own cube.", "… then have a go.", "… then it's your pen's turn.", "…
  then try it.", "… then roll your own.", "… then wrap yours.", "… then draw a can of your own.", "…
  then pour one for yourself.", "… then stack your own.", "… then set up your own.", and all the
  Fruits and Food & Treats ones ("… then pick up your pen.", "… then it's over to you.", "… then
  you try.", "… then it's your go.", "… then build yours.", "Watch it grow, …").
- Step sentences: "Draw one line along the middle of the leaf. Stop before the tip.", "Start at the
  top. Draw a big letter C down to the bottom.", "Go back to the top. Draw the same curve the other
  way, like a mirror."
- Outros: "That's the Fruits path, finished.", "… that's the Forms path done.", "… that's Food and
  Treats finished.", "It looks like you could pick it up.", "What flavor is inside?"

## The scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep the
voice of these lines and adapt the count. Anything changed is reported back, and all ten are read
side by side before the creator reviews them.

### pine-tree

(Picture: three tiers, the top one a whole triangle, the two below it hanging from the base line
above; rounded corners; a three-sided trunk. Trace: `s1` closed triangle, `s2` and `s3` open
three-sided tiers, `s4` the trunk. Cut every tier at its two base corners so each straight edge is
its own line: left slope from the top down, right slope from the top down, base left to right.)

- intro: "A pine tree is easier than it looks. It's three triangles stacked like hats on a short trunk. See them pile up, then plant one on your page."
- top: "The tip of the pine is a small triangle. Slant down to the left, down to the right, then join them."
- middle: "Now a wider layer. Start on the flat line, a little way in, and slant out both ways. Close it underneath."
- bottom: "One more layer, the widest of all. It hangs from the line above, the same way."
- trunk: "The trunk is a short box under the middle. Two lines down and one across."
- colors: "Color the top layer light green." / "Color the middle layer green." / "Make the bottom layer dark green." / "Color the trunk brown."
- outro: "Three layers and a trunk, and your pine stands tall. Draw a few more and you'll have a forest."

### tulip

(Picture: a cup with three points, a narrow two-line stem closed at the foot, two tall leaves from
the foot of the stem, one floating vein each. Trace: bloom `s1` closed, 1203 long: cut it at the two
outer tips so the zigzag top is one line and the round cup is cut again at the bottom middle. Stem
`s2`; left leaf `s3` + `s4`, right leaf `s5`: every leaf edge runs from the stem out to the tip.)

- intro: "When tulips pop up, spring is here. Ours is a cup with three points on a long stem, with two tall leaves. Watch it open, then grow one yourself."
- crown: "The tulip's top edge is a zigzag with three points. Make the middle point the tallest."
- cup-left: "Now the cup. From the left point, curve down and around to the bottom middle."
- cup-right: "From the right point, curve down to meet it. The bloom is closed."
- stem: "The stem drops from under the cup. Two long lines, close together, joined at the foot."
- left-leaf: "A tall leaf grows from the foot of the stem. Curve out and up to a sharp tip, then come back down."
- right-leaf: "The leaf on the right is a little shorter. Out, up to its tip, and back to the stem."
- veins: "Each leaf gets one line down its middle, bending the way the leaf bends."
- colors: "Color the bloom pink." / "Color the stem green." / "Make both leaves dark green."
- outro: "Your tulip is open, and this one never needs watering."

### cactus

(Picture: a tall post with a round top, two L-shaped arms in light green butting against its
unbroken sides, six floating dashes: four on the post, one on each arm. Trace with
`--max-outline-width 40`, or one dash is read as a color. Body `s1` closed, 1985 long: cut at the
top middle and the two bottom corners. Each arm is one open line of 800 to 900: cut it at the top
of its round tip, so the arm is an inside line and an outside line, both starting at the post.)

- intro: "Far out in the desert stands a saguaro cactus, holding up two arms. It's a tall post, two bent arms and a few spines. Look how the arms bend, then draw yours."
- body-left: "The cactus is a tall post with a round top. From the bottom left, go straight up and curve over to the middle."
- body-right: "Carry on over the top and straight down the right side. A short flat line closes the bottom."
- left-arm: "The left arm is a letter L. From the post, go out, turn up and round over the tip."
- left-arm-outside: "Then the outside of the L: from the post again, out, around the elbow and up to the tip." (one step with the line above when the two fit together: "The left arm is a letter L with a round tip. Draw its inside line, then its outside line.")
- right-arm: "The right arm sits higher and is shorter. Two lines again, inside first, both from the post."
- spines-body: "Spines are short slanted dashes. Put four down the post, leaning left, then right, in turn."
- spines-arms: "One more dash on each arm."
- colors: "Color the post green." / "Color both arms light green."
- outro: "Two arms up and six spines, and nobody got prickled. That saguaro is yours."

### mushroom

(Picture: red dome closed by a rim line that bows slightly upward, three outlined cream ovals, a gray stem that
reaches up to the rim, a cream underside in two halves either side of the stem, three floating gill
lines in each half. Trace: dome `s1` open, 1295: cut at the top. `s6` is the rim line and the left
underside curve in one line: cut it at the left end of the rim. `s10` is the right underside curve.
Stem `s5` is one three-sided line: cut at the foot so each side runs from the rim down.)

- intro: "Here is the mushroom from every fairy tale, red on top with pale spots. We'll peek under the cap too. Watch where the gills go, then it's time for yours."
- cap-left: "The cap is a wide dome. From the top, curve down to the left like half a rainbow."
- cap-right: "Now the other half of the rainbow, from the top down to the right."
- rim: "Join the two ends with a long line that lifts a little in the middle."
- spots: "Three spots float on the cap. A big oval up high, then a smaller one on each side."
- stem: "The stem hangs from the middle of the rim. It widens on the way down and ends in a round foot."
- underside: "Under the rim, draw a shallow curve from each end of the cap in to the stem."
- gills-left: "Gills are short lines under the cap. Draw three on the left, fanning out like umbrella ribs."
- gills-right: "Three more on the right, fanning the other way."
- colors: "Color the cap red, around the spots." / "Color the three spots cream, and the underside too." / "Color the stem gray."
- outro: "Spots on top and gills underneath. Your mushroom belongs in a storybook."

### big-oak

(Picture: one brown shape, a flared trunk dividing into three round-tipped branches, in front of a
green canopy of nine bumps whose lower edge meets the trunk on both sides. Trace: trunk and
branches `s2` closed, 2468: cut at the two bottom corners, the three branch tips and the two sharp
V's between branches. Canopy `s1` open, 2317: cut at every dip between two bumps.)

- intro: "An oak can live for hundreds of years. Ours has a thick trunk, three strong branches and a treetop like a big green cloud. See it spread out, then raise your own."
- trunk-left: "Begin at the ground on the left. Go up the trunk, then swing out and up to the left branch's tip."
- trunk-right: "Now a flat line for the foot. From its end, go up the trunk and swing out to the right."
- left-branch: "Round off the left branch's tip and come back down its inside to a sharp V."
- middle-branch: "The middle branch is a tall thumb. From the V, go up, round the top, and down to a second V."
- right-branch: "From that V, run up the inside of the right branch and round its tip to close it."
- canopy-left: "The treetop is a ring of big bumps. Start at the trunk on the left and bump your way up to the top."
- canopy-right: "Carry on down the right side, bump by bump, until you reach the trunk again."
- colors: "Color the treetop green, all around the branches." / "Color the trunk and branches brown."
- outro: "From the ground to the highest bump, that oak is yours. Imagine the shade under it."

### windowsill-garden

(Picture: a gray ledge; three pots standing apart on it, each two slanted sides under a rim box; a
cactus with two arms and three tiny dashes; a tulip with no veins; a bonsai with an S trunk, one
left branch and two smooth domes. Trace with `--max-colours 12`. Every color is one area across the
whole scene, so the color steps go by color, not by plant. Six drawing steps, six color steps.)

- intro: "Three plants from this path now share one sunny ledge: a cactus, a tulip and a bonsai, each in its own pot. Watch the pots line up, then start your garden."
- ledge: "Everything stands on a ledge. Draw a long, flat box across the lower part of your page."
- pots: "Three pots stand on it. Each is two lines leaning in toward the ledge. Make the right one low and wide."
- rims: "Give every pot a rim: a thin box lying across the top of its two sides."
- cactus: "A cactus grows in the left pot: a round-topped post, two L arms, and three dashes for spines."
- tulip: "In the middle pot, a tall stem with a three-point cup on top, then a leaf up each side."
- bonsai: "The last pot holds a bonsai. Wiggle its trunk up like a letter S, add a branch, and cap each end with a dome."
- colors: "Color the cactus post, the tulip stem and the big bonsai dome green." / "Dark green for the tulip leaves and the small dome. Light green for the cactus arms." / "Color the tulip pink. Brown is for the bonsai trunk and the first pot's rim." / "Orange for the first pot and the middle pot's rim. Yellow for the middle pot." / "The bonsai pot is blue with a purple rim." / "Color the ledge gray."
- outro: "Your windowsill is full, and so is the Plants path. Everything on that ledge was grown by you."

### palm-tree

(Second picture, 2026-09-20: a leaning trunk with a small round cap and four floating ring lines; five
drooping leaves, each a smooth arch on top and saw teeth underneath. The teeth are not the same number
on every leaf (three or four), so Lina never counts them. Trace: 32 lines, because every tooth edge
comes as its own short piece and `s13` (2288) runs the trunk together with the lower leaves' arches:
cut `s13` where the leaves leave the trunk, and join each leaf's tooth pieces into one zigzag that runs
from the tip back to the trunk. Each leaf is then two lines: the arch out, the teeth back.)

- intro: "Think of a beach. There's a palm tree on it, leaning toward the sea. Watch its leaves spill over like a fountain, then draw the palm on your own beach."
- trunk-left: "The trunk leans to the right. From the ground, draw one long curve up to the top."
- trunk-right: "Now a flat foot to the right. From its end, draw a second curve up, closer at the top."
- rings: "Four short curved lines cross the trunk like little smiles. They stop before the edges."
- top-leaf: "A palm leaf is an arch with a saw under it. Arch up and over to the right, then zigzag back."
- upper-left-leaf: "Now a long leaf to the left. Arch out to its tip, then saw your way back to the trunk."
- upper-right-leaf: "Its partner reaches out to the right, the same way: arch first, teeth second."
- lower-left-leaf: "A shorter leaf droops under the left one. Curve out and down, then zigzag home."
- lower-right-leaf: "One last drooping leaf on the right finishes the crown."
- colors: "Color the top leaf and the two drooping ones green." / "The two long side leaves are dark green." / "Color the trunk brown."
- outro: "Five leaves and a leaning trunk. You can almost hear the waves."

### sunflower

(Second picture, 2026-09-20: the whole plant. A brown center, eight yellow front petals, eight orange
tips peeking between them, a two-line stem that runs off toward the bottom with no foot line, two
heart-shaped leaves on short stalks with one floating vein each. Trace: 23 lines; every front petal
is one arch from the center back to the center, every back tip one tent. The center is 707 long: cut
it top and bottom. Twelve steps is the ceiling, so each leaf's vein is drawn with its leaf.)

- intro: "A sunflower turns its face to follow the sun. Ours has a big brown middle, yellow petals in front and orange ones peeking from behind. See the layers go on, then grow your own."
- center: "The middle of a sunflower is a big circle. Draw its left half, then its right half."
- front-petals-1: "Front petals are pointed like candle flames. Draw four from the circle: up, right, down and left."
- front-petals-2: "Put one more flame in each gap. That makes eight."
- back-petals-1: "Behind every gap a petal peeks out. It's a little tent: up to a point and down again. Draw four."
- back-petals-2: "Four more tents fill the other gaps, all the way around."
- stem: "The stem comes down from the bottom petal, two long lines side by side."
- left-leaf: "A leaf sits on a short stalk on the left. Draw the stalk, a wide heart with a point, and a line inside."
- right-leaf: "Lower down on the right, a second stalk, heart and inside line."
- colors: "Color the front petals yellow." / "The peeking petals are orange." / "Color the middle brown." / "Green for the stem, dark green for the two leaves."
- outro: "Sixteen petals, and every one of them yours. It's facing the sun already."

### rose

(Second picture, 2026-09-20: a side view. A pink round bump on top holding a swirl; a wide front
petal lying across two side petals that curl to points; a curved two-line stem; two pointed leaves
with one floating vein each. All three petals are red, told apart by their outlines. Trace: 10 lines;
`s2` (1563) is the outer edge of both side petals in one line: cut it at the two curled tips and
where it passes behind the front petal, so each side petal is its own line or two. What is in front
is drawn first, so no line ever has to stop at a line that is not there yet.)

- intro: "A rose looks hard until you know its secret: a little swirl on top, and three petals hugging it. Watch them wrap around, then draw a rose for someone."
- heart: "The heart of the rose starts as a hook, like a question mark with no dot."
- swirl: "Inside the hook, wind one swirl from the middle outward, like a snail shell."
- front-petal: "The front petal is a wide heart with no notch. Start where the hook ends and loop all the way around."
- left-petal: "A petal hugs the heart from the left. Rise from the front petal, swell out, and curl to a point on top."
- right-petal: "Its twin hugs from the right, curling the other way."
- stem: "The stem curves down from under the bloom, two lines that bend together."
- left-leaf: "A pointed leaf grows up and out to the left, with one line inside it."
- right-leaf: "Lower down, a second leaf reaches right. Give it its inside line too."
- colors: "Color the three petals red." / "The heart of the rose is pink." / "Color the stem dark green." / "Color both leaves green."
- outro: "A swirl, three petals and a stem. That's a rose, and no thorns on yours."

### bonsai

(The creator kept the FIRST picture, 2026-09-20: an S-shaped trunk with a branch each way, three
flat-bottomed pads with three bumps each, a blue pot with a purple rim and two purple feet. Trace: 11
lines. The trunk comes as four edges that make one tour: up the left from the pot to the left pad,
from there along the branch and up to the top pad, down the other side and out to the right pad, and
back down to the pot. Reverse them so the tour runs that way. Pads: cut at the dips between bumps and
at the two bottom corners. Pot body: cut at its two bottom corners.)

- intro: "A bonsai is a real tree kept tiny in a tray, trimmed for years until it looks old and windswept. Pot first, then the twisty trunk, then the green. Watch it take its time, then shape your own."
- pot-rim: "The pot is low and wide. Begin with its rim, a long thin box with round corners."
- pot-body: "Under the rim, two short lines lean in to a flat bottom. Then tuck two little square feet below."
- trunk-left: "The trunk wiggles like a letter S. From the rim, wind up the left and out along the low branch."
- trunk-middle: "From that branch's end, curve back in and climb to where the top will sit."
- trunk-right: "Come down the other side and swing out to the right branch. Then wind all the way back to the pot."
- top-pad: "Each layer of green has a flat bottom and three bumps on top. Sit the biggest on the trunk's tip."
- left-pad: "A smaller layer rests on the low left branch. Flat line first, then bump, bump, bump."
- right-pad: "One more on the right branch, a little higher than the left one."
- colors: "Color the top layer green." / "The two side layers are dark green." / "Color the trunk brown." / "The pot is blue, with a purple rim and feet."
- outro: "Years of patient trimming, and you did it in minutes. That little tree is yours."
