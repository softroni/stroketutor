# Space: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so ten lessons in a row do not say the same sentence ten
times, and do not sound like ten different teachers either. This is the plan for the `space` lessons
(Core level, 2026-09-21), written from the pictures as they came back, not from the prompts. The
models to follow are the creator's own Strawberry and the scripts in
[fruits-words.md](fruits-words.md), [food-treats-words.md](food-treats-words.md),
[forms-words.md](forms-words.md), [plants-words.md](plants-words.md),
[sky-weather-words.md](sky-weather-words.md), [wheels-words.md](wheels-words.md),
[on-the-water-words.md](on-the-water-words.md) and [in-the-air-words.md](in-the-air-words.md).

Space is the path of round things and machines, so Lina keeps her feet on the ground: no rockets
"blasting into the unknown", no wishing on stars. She names the shape a child already knows (a fat
letter C, a circle, a bullet, a lens, a flat ring) and then hangs the small parts on it. Nothing in
these pictures is far away or floating in the dark; every drawing sits on white paper, and she never
mentions a sky, because none of them have one.

**Where a picture differs from its prompt, the words follow the picture.** The creator redrew several
of them:

- `comet` has a **flame-shaped tail** with pointed licks and an orange flame inside it, not a
  three-tip flag with two streak lines.
- `satellite` has a **yellow can-shaped body with a gray dome at each end** and one blue circle on its
  front, dark gray arms, panels split into **six panes each**, and a small yellow ball on the dish's arm.
- `mars-rover` has **five wheels, each hanging from its own leg**, a dish on the left and the camera
  on the right. The plan's objective still says six wheels and an antenna: it needs rewording, and
  the lesson is built to the picture.
- `space-shuttle` sits on **three engine nozzles with three flames**, has a dark gray nose and a blue
  window, and its boosters stand beside the body rather than on the wings.
- `solar-system` has three orbits that are **nested but not centered on the sun**, as a real orbit
  chart is drawn.
- `crescent-moon`, `planet-with-rings`, `star`, `rocket` and `ufo` came back as the prompts asked.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first.** "The moon is a fat letter C." "Craters are just circles."
   A step never opens with only "Start at the top". Then, if it helps, where the pen starts and one
   familiar picture (a letter C, a bullet, a lens, a can, a slab, a flat ring, a teardrop).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no jargon, no sizes.
4. **Space words are kept to a few and used the same way everywhere:** "crater", "ring" (the whole
   flat band) and "band", "point" (of the star), "tail", "nose cone", "fin", "dome", "beam", "panel",
   "dish", "deck", "leg", "mast", "booster", "nozzle", "orbit". Never "orbital", "thruster",
   "payload", "asteroid", "galaxy". "Comet" and "satellite" are said once each, in their own lesson.
5. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now", "Next", "Then", "Last". About one step in three.
6. **No sentence is shared word for word with another lesson of any path, except a plain color
   step.** Circles come back in nine of these ten lessons, so each script words them differently:
   craters, lights, wheels, planets, a window and a ball are never introduced the same way twice.
7. **Color steps name the thing and the color.** `svg from-image` gives one area per color, so a
   lesson has one color step per color, or two or three small colors together; the step names every
   part that color covers, largest first. Plain is the default; at most one per lesson is framed
   differently. Lina says "gray", "cream", "dark gray" (the shuttle's nose and nozzles, the
   satellite's arms: both off-palette grays the pictures brought with them).
8. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** Outros are one or
   two short sentences about their drawing, never about getting it right.
9. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the outer curve", "Color the panels". Titles may repeat
   across lessons; they are labels, not speech.
10. **Drawing rules still hold** (see the author-lesson skill): a big closed curve is split where a
    pen would lift (the moon at its two tips, a round body at its nose and its foot, a saucer at its
    two points, an orbit at the far side and the near side); small closed shapes (a crater, a light,
    a window, a wheel, a planet) stay whole; one thing is finished before the next; a line that is
    fixed to something starts where it joins it (a leg from the deck, a mast from the deck, a stalk
    from the body, a flame from the nozzle); at most six lines in a step.

## Phrases already used by published lessons (do not reuse)

Everything listed under the same heading in [on-the-water-words.md](on-the-water-words.md),
[wheels-words.md](wheels-words.md), [plants-words.md](plants-words.md) and
[forms-words.md](forms-words.md), and every script in the eight earlier words documents. In particular:

- Openers: "Today we're drawing …", "In this lesson we're drawing …", "Hi, Lina here.", "Think of a
  beach.", "Think of a party hat.", "Flash.", "Surf's up.", "Rumble, rumble.", "Psst.", "Lunch is
  served.", "Pizza night.", "Two scoops, please.", "Who wants a donut?", "Roll the dice.", "Beep beep,
  here comes …", "Make way, …", "Ahoy.", "All aboard.", "Dive, dive, dive.", "Hold on tight.",
  "Ready, set, go.", "Vroom.", "Everything from this path meets in one picture …".
- Hand-overs: every "… then it's your turn.", "… then grab your pen.", "… then have a go.", "… then
  build yours.", "… then draw your own.", "… then take yours for a spin.", "… then hoist your own
  sails.", "… then board your own.", "… then you take over.", and the rest of the list in
  [on-the-water-words.md](on-the-water-words.md). **New to this path:** "… then count down and go.",
  "… then hang one over your own night.", "… then set yours spinning.", "… then send one up.",
  "… then park yours on Mars.", "… then light the engines.", "… then let one fly past.", "… then give
  it a ring of its own.", "… then make five sharp points.", "… then switch yours on."
- Step sentences: "Draw a small circle right in the middle.", "Start at the top. Draw a big letter C
  down to the bottom.", "Go back to the top. Draw the same curve the other way, like a mirror."
- Outros: "It looks like you could pick it up.", "What flavor is inside?", "You can almost hear the
  waves.", "Hold on to your hat.", every "That's the … path, finished/done."

## The scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep the
voice of these lines and adapt the count. Anything changed is reported back, and all ten are read
side by side before the creator reviews them.

### crescent-moon

(Picture as prompted: a fat yellow crescent opening to the right, three gray craters of three sizes
down its thick side, each with yellow all around it. Traces clean: one closed 2,400-unit outline and
three circles. Cut the outline at the upper tip and the lower tip, so the outer curve and the inner
curve are each one line.)

- intro: "A crescent moon is a circle with a bite taken out of it. Watch the bite go in, then hang one over your own night."
- outer: "The moon is a fat letter C. From the top tip, curve left and all the way down to the bottom tip."
- inner: "Now the bite. Come back up from the bottom tip, curving right, to meet the top tip."
- crater-top: "Craters are just circles. Put a small one high up, inside the thick side."
- crater-big: "The biggest crater goes below it, where the moon is widest."
- crater-small: "One more under that, smaller than the first two."
- moon: "Color the moon yellow."
- craters: "Color all three craters gray."
- outro: "There's your moon, craters and all. It's the same one you can see from your window."

### planet-with-rings

(Picture as prompted: an orange planet with a wide yellow ring, in front of the planet below the
middle and hidden behind it above. The trace runs the planet's top curve and the ring's whole outer
edge together as one closed line (s1): cut it where the planet's edge meets the ring on the left,
about 266,436, and on the right, about 680,322. The planet's lower curve (s5) and the ring's inner
edge (s2) are their own lines; two short lines close the hole on each side.)

- intro: "This planet wears a ring, and the ring goes behind it at the top and in front at the bottom. Watch which lines stop, then give one a ring of its own."
- planet-top: "Start with the planet. Curve over the top, from the ring on the left round to the ring on the right."
- planet-bottom: "Under the ring, close the planet with a wide curve, like the bottom of a ball."
- ring-outer: "The ring is a flat band. Draw its outside edge, a long oval that leans up to the right."
- ring-inner: "Now the inside edge. It crosses in front of the planet and follows the outside all the way round."
- ring-ends: "Two short lines close the hole, one on each side of the planet."
- planet: "Color the planet orange."
- ring: "Color the ring yellow."
- outro: "A planet with a ring around its middle. Yours is tilted, just like the real one."

### star

(Picture as prompted: one flat yellow five-point star with softly rounded corners, no lines inside.
It traces as a single closed 2,550-unit line. The objective asks for it point by point, so cut it at
the five inner corners: each piece then runs up one side of a point, round the tip and down the
other side. Start with the top point and work clockwise.)

- intro: "A star is five points, each one two lines meeting at a tip. Take them one at a time, then make five sharp points."
- point-top: "First the top point. Go up to the tip and straight back down the other side."
- point-right: "Next, the point reaching out to the right. Same shape, lying on its side."
- point-lower-right: "Now down to the leg on the right. Out to its tip, then back up."
- point-lower-left: "The left leg is a mirror of it. Out to the tip, then back."
- point-left: "Last, the point reaching left. Close the star where you began."
- star: "Color the star yellow."
- outro: "Five points, one star. Count them round the edge and see."

### comet

(Picture: the creator's own. A blue ball at the lower right with a long flame-shaped tail streaming
up to the left, its edges cut into pointed licks, and a second orange flame inside the yellow one.
The trace cuts the tail's edges into pieces at the licks; line s11 runs the tail's lower edge and
then carries on all the way around the ball, so cut it at about 670,812, where the edge reaches the
ball. The ball is drawn first because every other line ends on it.)

- intro: "A comet is a ball of ice with a tail of fire behind it. Ours is coming right at us. See how the tail is built, then let one fly past."
- ball: "The comet's head is a plain circle, down at the right. Draw it whole."
- tail-top: "The tail streams up to the left. From the top of the ball, run its upper edge out."
- tail-licks-top: "The edge is cut into licks. Add the pointed ones, on out to the far tip."
- tail-bottom: "Come back to the ball and run the lower edge out the same way."
- tail-licks-bottom: "Give that edge its licks too, pointing back the way the comet came."
- flame: "A second flame sits inside the tail, the same shape but smaller."
- tail: "Color the tail yellow."
- inner: "Color the flame inside it orange."
- head: "Color the ball blue."
- outro: "Your comet is halfway across the page. Nothing in space moves faster."

(As built, 9 steps: each edge of the tail is a chain — the long run from the ball, then its licks out
to the far tip — so "to the far tip" moved from the edge step to the licks step. The orange flame
inside has no ink along its lower right, where orange simply meets yellow, so it is an open shape of
four lines; that is the picture, not a gap. The color order stays the script's, tail then flame then
ball, which puts the larger ball last.)

### rocket

(Picture as prompted: a gray bullet-shaped body with a red nose cone above one straight line, a blue
round window, two red fins and an orange flame with a yellow flame inside it. Traces clean, seven
lines. Cut the body at the nose tip and at the middle of its bottom edge, so each side is one line.)

- intro: "Here's a rocket on the pad, fins down, flame lit. Build it from the nose down, then count down and go."
- body-left: "Start at the tip of the nose. Draw the left side, curving down to the bottom."
- body-right: "Back to the tip. Take the right side down the same way, and close the bottom."
- nose-line: "One straight line across, near the top. Everything above it is the nose cone."
- window: "The window is a circle in the middle of the body, with room all around it."
- fins: "A fin goes on each side at the bottom, like two swept-back wings."
- flame: "Under the rocket, hang a flame: wide at the top, down to one soft point."
- inner-flame: "A smaller flame sits inside it, the same teardrop shape."
- body: "Color the body gray."
- red: "Color the nose cone and both fins red."
- flames: "Color the flame orange, and the little one inside it yellow."
- window-color: "Color the window blue."
- outro: "Your rocket is ready. Five, four, three, two, one."

### ufo

(Picture as prompted: a gray saucer shaped like a lens, a blue half-circle dome on top, three orange
lights in a row and a solid yellow beam widening below. Traces clean, six lines. Cut the saucer at
its left point and its right point, so the top edge and the bottom edge are each one line.)

- intro: "A flying saucer is two curves that meet in a point at each end. Ours has its light on underneath. Watch it come together, then switch yours on."
- saucer-top: "The saucer is shaped like a lens. Draw the top curve, from the left point to the right point."
- saucer-bottom: "The underside is a shallower curve. Come back from the right point to close it."
- dome: "A dome sits on top, in the middle: half a circle standing on the saucer."
- lights: "Three round lights go in a row along the side, evenly spread."
- beam: "The beam drops straight down from the middle: two slanted sides and a flat bottom."
- saucer: "Color the saucer gray."
- dome-color: "Color the dome blue, and the three lights orange."
- beam-color: "Color the beam yellow."
- outro: "Something is being looked at down there. Your saucer found it first."

### satellite

(Picture: the creator's own, tilted across the page. A yellow can-shaped body with a gray dome at
the top and another at the bottom, one blue circle on its front, a dark gray arm out of each side to
a blue panel split into six panes, and a dish on a short arm above, with a small yellow ball on a
stalk beside it. Twenty lines: the busiest picture on the path. Panels take their outline and their
three divider lines in one step each. Nothing here needs cutting.)

- intro: "A satellite is a can with two wings of solar panels and a dish that listens. Build it from the body out, then send one up."
- body: "The body is a can standing on its end, leaning to the left. Draw its right side."
- body-2: "Come back to the top and take the left side down to meet it."
- caps: "A dome caps each end. Draw the line across the top, then the curve around the bottom."
- port: "One round port sits on the front of the can."
- panel-left: "Now the left wing. A short arm holds a long panel, split into six panes."
- panel-right: "The right wing matches it: arm, panel, then the lines that make six panes."
- dish: "A short arm lifts the dish above the body. The dish is a shallow bowl, tipped up to the right."
- feed: "A thin arm reaches out of the dish to a little ball."
- body-color: "Color the body and the little ball yellow."
- panels: "Color both panels blue, and the round port too."
- gray-parts: "The domes and the dish are gray. The arms are a darker gray."
- outro: "Your satellite is out there listening. Which way is its dish pointing?"

(As built, 11 steps: the can's 996-unit outline was cut at its top middle (506,366) and its bottom
middle (528,718) into a right side and a left side, which cleared the `long-stroke` warning; the
little ball is the same yellow as the body, so it is named in that color step; the bottom dome's
edge is a curve, not a straight line. The complexity warning against the UFO stays: this is the
busiest picture on the path.)

### mars-rover

(Picture: the creator's own, and not what the objective says. A long orange deck, **five** gray
wheels each hanging from its own slanted leg, a gray dish on a short mast at the left, and a gray
camera box with a blue lens on a tall mast at the right. In the trace every wheel is fused to its
leg, and the deck's edges run into the masts: cut each leg from its wheel at about 178,652 / 325,650
/ 662,653 / 816,655, cut the right mast off the deck's top edge at about 758,500, and cut the dish's
mast off at about 272,506. The middle wheel (s12) is already a circle on its own.)

- intro: "A rover drives around Mars on its own, taking pictures. This one rides on five wheels. Watch it get built, then park yours on Mars."
- deck: "The deck is a long slab with round corners. Draw it lying across the middle."
- legs: "Five legs slant down from underneath, one for each wheel."
- wheels: "A wheel hangs from every leg. Draw all five as plain circles."
- mast: "At the right, a tall mast stands up from the deck."
- camera: "The camera is a box on top of the mast, lying on its side."
- lens: "Its lens is a circle inside the box, near the front."
- dish-mast: "At the left, a short mast leans back with a dish on it."
- dish: "The dish is a bowl seen from the side, open to the right."
- deck-color: "Color the deck orange."
- gray-parts: "The five wheels, the camera box and the dish are gray."
- lens-color: "Color the lens blue."
- outro: "Five wheels, one camera, ready to roll. What would you send it to look at?"

### space-shuttle

(Picture: the creator's own. A cream body with a dark gray nose and a blue window, gray wings
sweeping out and down, an orange booster standing on each side, and three gray nozzles under the
body with an orange flame under each. Cut the body at the nose tip and at the middle of its bottom
edge. Two 21-unit specks where the wings meet the body (s8, s9) are trace crumbs: delete them.)

- intro: "A space shuttle goes up on boosters and glides home on its wings. Watch it stack up, then light the engines."
- body-left: "The shuttle's body is a tall bullet. Draw the left side down from the nose."
- body-right: "Bring the right side down to match, and close it along the bottom."
- nose-line: "One curved line across the top marks off the nose."
- window: "Under it, the window is a small rounded shape lying flat."
- wings: "A wing sweeps out and down from each side. Then it curves back up to the body."
- boosters: "A booster stands on each wing: a tall tube with a pointed top. One line across each, near the top."
- nozzles: "Three nozzles sit under the shuttle, one in the middle and one each side."
- nozzle-bands: "Each nozzle gets a line across it."
- flames: "A flame comes out of every nozzle, three pointed teardrops."
- body-color: "Color the body cream."
- orange-parts: "Color both boosters orange, and all three flames."
- dark-parts: "The wings are gray, and the nose and nozzles darker gray."
- window-color: "Color the window blue, and the middle of each flame yellow."
- outro: "That's a shuttle ready to fly. It's the wings that bring it back down."

(As built, 12 steps. Each band line is folded into the part it crosses, so `nozzle-bands` joins
`nozzles` and `booster-bands` joins `boosters`. **Each wing is one line that finishes in its own
step**: out and down from the body, round the outer tip, and back up to the body's side. The curve
that closes it is the booster's foot, which the trace had given to the booster, so it was cut out of
the booster's closed path and moved here; the 12-unit stub of the booster's side that came with it
was trimmed off, or it showed as a spike on the wing. Each booster is then one line running foot to
foot, up one side, over the tip and down the other, standing on a curve already drawn. Of the two
grays, the darker one covers the nose and the nozzles' upper parts and the palette gray covers the
wings and the strip under each nozzle's band.)

### solar-system

(Picture: the creator's own. A yellow sun near the middle, three orbits drawn as flat ovals nested
one inside the other but not centered on the sun, and three planets sitting in a gap in their own
orbit: red on the inner one, blue on the middle one, orange on the outer one. Traces beautifully:
each orbit is one open line broken at its planet. The orbits are long, so cut each one into a far
half and a near half. This is the last lesson of the path.)

- intro: "Planets circle the sun on paths called orbits, and each one looks like a flat oval. Draw them ring by ring, then set yours spinning."
- sun: "The sun goes first: one circle, a little left of the middle."
- orbit-inner-far: "The first orbit loops around it. Draw the far side, from the gap on the right."
- orbit-inner-near: "Bring the near side back to the other end of the gap."
- planet-inner: "A small planet fills that gap. Draw it as a circle."
- orbit-middle: "The next orbit goes around the first, with its own gap at the upper left."
- planet-middle: "Sit a bigger planet in that gap."
- orbit-outer: "The last orbit wraps around them both. Leave its gap at the lower left."
- planet-outer: "The biggest planet goes there, the furthest out."
- sun-color: "Color the sun yellow."
- planets: "The inner planet is red, the middle one blue, the far one orange."
- outro: "Three planets, three orbits, one sun. That's the Space path, complete."
