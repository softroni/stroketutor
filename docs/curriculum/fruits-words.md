# Fruits: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so ten lessons in a row do not say the same sentence ten
times, and do not sound like ten different teachers either. This is the plan for the Fruits lessons
not yet published (2026-09-19): `watermelon-slice`, `orange-half`, `grapes`, `pineapple`,
`fruit-bowl`. The model to follow is the creator's own Strawberry.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first, whenever the learner could not guess it.** "Draw a pointed leaf
   up to the left." "To make a berry, curve down to a soft point." A step that opens with only
   "Start at the top" tells a child where the pen goes and not what they are making. Then, if it
   helps, where the pen starts and one familiar picture (a smile, a letter V, a clock).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no jargon, no sizes.
4. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now the other side", "Next", "Then". Not on every step;
   about one in three.
5. **No sentence is shared word for word with another Fruits lesson, except a plain color step**
   ("Color the leaf green." is fine anywhere). In particular these are used up: "Start at the top.
   Draw a big letter C down to the bottom.", "Go back to the top. Draw the same curve the other
   way, like a mirror.", "Draw one line along the middle of the leaf. Stop before the tip."
6. **Color steps name the thing and the color, and vary the frame a little across a lesson** when
   there are three or more: "Color the inside pink.", "Make the rind dark green.", "The seeds are
   black.", "Fill the thin stripe light green." Never more than one fancy one in a row; plain is
   the default.
7. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** Used already:
   "Today we're drawing …" (apple, cherries, banana), "In this lesson we're drawing …" (pear), "Hi,
   Lina here. Ready to draw …?" (strawberry). Hand-overs used: "Watch how it comes together, then
   it's your turn." (four times: retire it), "Watch the whole thing once, then grab your pen."
8. **Outros are one or two short sentences about their drawing**, never about getting it right.
   Used: "You did it. That's your very own …", "There they are …", "There it is …" (twice), "And
   that's a strawberry. Hold it up and have a good look. You made that!"
9. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the left side", "Color the rind". Titles may repeat
   across lessons; they are labels, not speech.

## The five scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep the
voice of these lines and adapt the count. Anything changed is reported back, and all five are read
side by side before any voice is recorded.

### watermelon-slice

- intro: "Here comes a summer favorite, a slice of watermelon. See how the slice is built, then you draw it."
- left-side: "The slice is a big triangle. From the tip, draw a straight line down to the left."
- right-side: "Now the other side. From the tip again, straight down to the right."
- bottom: "Close the slice with a curved bottom, like a wide smile."
- rind-top: "Next, the rind. Draw a curve from side to side, just above the bottom."
- rind-bottom: "Add one more curve under it. Now the rind has a stripe."
- seeds-top: "Time for seeds. Draw two little teardrops near the top."
- seeds-bottom: "Three more teardrops go in a row below them."
- flesh: "Color the inside pink."
- rind: "Make the rind dark green."
- stripe: "Fill the thin stripe light green."
- seeds: "The seeds are black."
- outro: "That's your watermelon slice. It looks cold and sweet, and you drew every seed."

### orange-half

(Picture chosen 2026-09-20 from three tried: eight outlined wedges that meet along shared spokes,
around a small center circle. It traces as a wheel a child can build: two big circles, the little
circle, eight spokes, then a rounded top on each segment. The star-center picture traced as a
tangle with no clean center, and the picture without wedge outlines left nothing to draw but
three circles. Traced with `--max-bend 30 --join-gap 4`.)

- intro: "Let's cut an orange in half and look inside. There's a wheel in there. Watch how it comes together, then it's your turn."
- outer circle, left half: "First the peel, a big circle. From the top, curve round the left to the bottom."
- outer circle, right half: "Back at the top, curve round the right. The circle closes at the bottom."
- inner circle: "A second circle sits just inside the first. Draw its left half." / "Then its right half. That ring is the peel."
- center: "Draw a small circle right in the middle."
- spokes: "Draw four spokes out from the little circle, like a plus sign. Stop short of the ring." / "Next, one spoke between each pair, like a letter X. Now there are eight."
- segment tops, clockwise from the top: "Each segment gets a rounded top. Curve from spoke tip to spoke tip down the right side." / "Carry on round like a clock, up the left side. The wheel is full."
- colors: "Color all eight segments golden yellow." / "Color the peel ring orange." / "Color the gaps cream, and the little circle too."
- outro: "One juicy orange half, all yours. Count the segments. There should be eight."

### grapes

(New picture, 2026-09-19: ten whole grapes that touch and never overlap, in rows of four, three,
two and one; a branching brown stalk; a three-point leaf with one vein; a single-line tendril; a
small light-purple shine on every grape. Each grape is one whole circle, drawn one at a time.)

- intro: "Grapes are just circles, lots of them, snuggled together. Watch the bunch fill up row by row, then pick up your pen."
- top row: "Start with the top row. Draw four circles side by side, just touching." (if split in two steps: "Start the top row. Draw two circles side by side, just touching." / "Two more circles finish the row of four.")
- second row: "The next row has three grapes. Each one sits in the dip between two above."
- third row and last grape: "Then a row of two." / "One last grape at the bottom makes the point of the bunch." (or together: "Then a row of two, and one last grape to make the point.")
- stalk: "The grapes hang from a stalk. Draw its two long edges up from the outside grapes." / "Little branches reach down to the other grapes. Draw the arches between them."
- leaf: "A vine leaf has three points. From the stalk, draw up to the first point and down again." / "Carry on over the other two points and back to the stalk." (one step if the quality check allows)
- vein: "Give the leaf one vein, from the top point down to the stalk."
- tendril: "Last, a curly tendril. From the stalk, swing out, loop once and wiggle away."
- colors: "Color every grape purple." / "The leaf is green." / "Color the stalk brown." / "Add a little shine on each grape in light purple. It has no outline."
- outro: "What a bunch. Every grape in it is yours."

### pineapple

- intro: "A pineapple looks tricky, but it's an oval, some crisscross lines and a spiky hat. Watch it grow, then it's over to you."
- body: "The body is a tall oval. From the top, curve down the left side." / "Now the right side, down to meet it at the bottom."
- crown: "Now the spiky hat. Draw the tall leaf in the middle first." / "Add a leaf on each side of it. They lean away." / "Two more leaves peek out behind those." / "The last two leaves curl out low, one each side."
- crosshatch: "Time for the crisscross. Draw the lines that lean one way, top to bottom." / "Cross them with lines leaning the other way. Now you have diamonds."
- colors: "Color the body yellow." / "The two low leaves are light green." / "Color the middle leaves green." / "Make the darkest leaves dark green." (adapt to the greens the trace gives, largest first)
- outro: "Spiky on top, diamonds all over. That's a fine pineapple you've drawn."

### fruit-bowl

- intro: "You know these fruits already. Now they all share one bowl. Watch who goes in front, then you try."
- bowl rim: "Begin with the bowl. Draw its front rim, a long curve like a smile."
- pear: "The pear sits in front. Draw its sides up from the rim, and a little stem."
- apple: "The apple leans on the pear. Curve it up from the rim and over to the pear." / "Give the apple its stem and a leaf."
- grapes: "Grapes on the right. Whole circles in front, then curves tucked behind." / "Finish the bunch and add its stem."
- banana: "A banana rests across the back. Draw its top curve from side to side." / "Add the parts of it that show between the fruit, then its ends and lines."
- bowl: "Back to the bowl. Draw the rim behind the fruit, the sides, and the little foot."
- colors: "Color the bowl blue, darker on the inside." / "The apple is red and the pear is light green." / "Color the banana yellow and the grapes purple." / "Stems and banana ends are brown. The leaf and grape stem are green." / "Color a soft gray shadow under the bowl. It has no outline."
- outro: "A whole bowl of fruit, and you drew every piece. That's the Fruits path, finished."
