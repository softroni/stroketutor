# On the Water: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so ten lessons in a row do not say the same sentence ten
times, and do not sound like ten different teachers either. This is the plan for the `on-the-water`
lessons (Core level, 2026-09-21), written from the pictures as they came back, not from the prompts.
The models to follow are the creator's own Strawberry and the scripts in
[fruits-words.md](fruits-words.md), [food-treats-words.md](food-treats-words.md),
[forms-words.md](forms-words.md), [plants-words.md](plants-words.md),
[sky-weather-words.md](sky-weather-words.md) and [wheels-words.md](wheels-words.md).

On the Water is the path where things float, so Lina talks like someone at the dock: the deck line
first, the hull hung under it, then whatever stands on the deck, and the small things last. A child
who has done two of these lessons should feel the pattern ("deck, hull, then what's on board") without
hearing the same sentence twice.

Where a picture differs from its prompt, the words follow the picture: the speedboat is red with an
outlined yellow lightning bolt and a domed windshield with one bar; the canoe's paddle leans across the
canoe, in front of it; the cruise ship has five and four windows, a slanted bridge window and a gray band
on its funnel; the harbor has five waves; the tug's stack band runs from side to side.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray, harbor.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first.** "The sail is a triangle." "A tugboat wears a thick bumper."
   A step never opens with only "Start at the top". Then, if it helps, where the pen starts and one
   familiar picture (a smile, a tub, a pill, a leaf, a letter J, an upside-down L, a cinnamon roll).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no jargon, no sizes. Boat words are kept to a few and
   used the same way everywhere: "deck" for the hull's top line, "hull" for the body, "bow" for the
   front (always on the right), "stern" for the back, "mast", "sail". Never "port", "starboard",
   "gunwale", "keel" is allowed once (longship).
4. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now", "Next", "Then", "Last". About one step in three.
5. **No sentence is shared word for word with another lesson of any path, except a plain color
   step.** Decks, hulls, masts, sails and plank lines come back in almost every lesson here, so each
   script words them differently; keep those differences.
6. **Color steps name the thing and the color.** `svg from-image` gives one area per color, so a
   lesson has one color step per color, or two small colors together; the step names every part that
   color covers, in the order to color them, largest first. Plain is the default; at most one per
   lesson is framed differently. Lina says "green" for leaf green, "dark green", "cream", "gray".
7. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** Outros are one or
   two short sentences about their drawing, never about getting it right.
8. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the deck", "Draw the hull", "Color the sail". Titles may
   repeat across lessons; they are labels, not speech.
9. **Drawing rules still hold** (see the author-lesson skill): a hull's outline is always cut into
   the deck line (left to right, to the tip of the bow) and the hull under it (from the stern's top
   corner, down, along and up to the bow); a big closed curve is split where a pen would lift (a
   corner, a tip; on a plain capsule, the middle of each end); small closed shapes (a window, a
   porthole, a shield, a flag) stay whole; one thing is finished before the next; at most six lines in
   a step; a line that is fixed to something starts where it joins it (a mast rises from the deck, a
   rod from the deck, a fishing line drops from the rod's tip); long level lines run left to right.

Everything listed under the same heading in [wheels-words.md](wheels-words.md),

  your page.", "… then grow your own.", "… then raise your own.", "… then start your garden.", "… then
  strike.", "… then make it rain.", "… then let it snow.", "… then spin your own.", "… then ride your

  the … path, finished/done."

## The scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep the
voice of these lines and adapt the count. Anything changed is reported back, and all scripts are read
side by side before the creator reviews them.

### sailboat

(Picture: blue hull whose deck line lifts into a pointed bow on the right, one floating plank line, a
bold mast standing on the deck, a cream triangle sail on the mast's left with a gap of white under it.
Traces clean: mast, the sail's two free sides as one line, hull, plank. Cut the hull at the stern's top
corner and at the tip of the bow. The mast runs bottom to top; the sail starts at its top corner on the
mast, slants down to the left and comes back level to the mast.)

- intro: "One hull, one mast, one sail: that's all a sailboat needs to catch the wind. See how few lines it takes, then launch your own."
- deck: "Every boat here starts with its deck. Draw a long line that lifts at the right, to the bow."
- hull: "Now the hull. From the left end, swing down and along. Rise to the bow's tip."
- plank: "A plank line runs along the side. Keep both ends inside the hull."
- mast: "Stand the mast on the deck: one tall, straight line going up."
- sail: "The sail is a triangle on the mast's left. Slant down from the top, then come straight back."
- colors: "Color the hull blue." / "Color the sail cream."
- outro: "Your sailboat is ready for a breeze. Which way is the wind blowing?"

### canoe

(Picture: green canoe with two upturned tips; a paddle leans across it, in front: a small brown
handle at the upper left, a straight shaft down to the right crossing the canoe's top edge, and a brown
leaf-shaped blade that covers part of the canoe's lower edge and pokes out below it. The trim line under
the top edge comes in two pieces, one each side of the shaft. The trace runs the canoe's outline and
the blade together as one 2,400-unit line (s3): cut it at both tips and where the canoe's lower edge
meets the blade on each side, then join so that the top edge, the blade, the lower-left edge and the
lower-right edge are each one line. The small pieces s3/s5/s6 near the crossing need sorting out in a
crop; check for stubs there. Handle and blade are one brown area.)

- intro: "A canoe goes wherever its paddle takes it. Ours has the paddle resting across it, in front. Watch which lines stop at the paddle, then push off with your pen."
- top: "A canoe is shaped like a long smile. Draw the top edge from tip to tip, dipping in the middle."
- shaft: "The paddle leans across the canoe. Draw its small handle, then a straight shaft down past the edge."
- blade: "The blade is a long, rounded leaf on the end of the shaft."
- bottom-left: "Now the canoe's belly. From the left tip, curve down and along until you bump into the blade."
- bottom-right: "Carry on past the blade, and rise to the right tip."
- trim: "Two trim lines follow the top edge, one on each side of the paddle."
- colors: "Color the canoe green." / "Color the blade and the handle brown."
- outro: "Canoe and paddle, waiting at the water's edge. Where would you paddle to?"

### tugboat

(Picture: deep red hull with a flat bottom, hanging under a long gray bumper shaped like a pill that
rises toward the bow and sticks out past both ends; a yellow cabin with soft top corners standing on
the bumper; one round blue window toward the bow; an orange smokestack on the roof's left with one band
line from side to side. Traces clean. The bumper is a closed line 1,800 around: cut it at the middle of
each rounded end into a top line (left to right) and a bottom line. The hull is an open tub of 1,100:
cut it where the flat bottom ends and the bow's curve begins.)

- intro: "Tugboats are small, but they push the biggest ships around. That's why they wear a bumper. Watch this stubby one take shape, then put yours to work."
- bumper-top: "A tugboat wears a thick bumper all around. Draw its top line, rising a little at the bow."
- bumper-bottom: "Round the end and come back underneath. The bumper closes up like a long pill."
- hull-side: "The hull hangs under the bumper like a deep tub. Drop down on the left and run along the flat bottom."
- hull-bow: "Then sweep up in one big curve to meet the bumper at the bow."
- cabin: "The cabin stands on the bumper. Go up, across with soft corners, and down again."
- window: "One round window looks out toward the bow."
- stack: "A smokestack stands on the roof: up, across, down. Add a band near its top."
- colors: "Color the hull red." / "Color the cabin yellow." / "Color the bumper gray." / "Color the smokestack orange and the window blue."
- outro: "Short, round and strong. What big ship will your tugboat push today?"

### speedboat

(Picture: red wedge of a hull with a short stern on the left and a sharp nose on the right; a low blue
windshield dome on the deck with one slanted bar; a yellow lightning bolt along the side, drawn as an
outline whose left end is closed by the stern and whose right end is a point with a short tail line;
three looping splash lines behind the stern. Not the prompt's boat, and better. Traces clean: the
bolt's top and bottom edges are two open lines running from the stern to the point. Cut the hull at the
stern's top corner and at the nose. Splash loops start near the boat and curl away.)

- intro: "Hold on tight. This speedboat has a lightning bolt down its side and a splash flying up behind it. See it race past, then start your engine."
- deck: "A speedboat is a long wedge. Draw its top edge from the back, rising gently to a sharp nose."
- hull: "Go down the short stern. Then sweep along the bottom to the nose."
- windshield: "The windshield is a low dome on the deck. Add one slanted bar inside it."
- bolt-top: "A lightning bolt runs down the side. Draw its top edge: along, a quick zigzag, then on to the point."
- bolt-bottom: "Draw the bolt's lower edge the same way, so both meet at the point."
- splash: "Water flies up behind the boat. Draw three loops that curl over: big, medium and small."
- colors: "Color the hull red." / "The bolt is yellow." / "Color the windshield blue."
- outro: "That's one fast boat. Can you feel the spray?"

### fishing-boat

(Picture: dark green hull whose deck rises into a pointed bow, one floating plank line, a yellow cabin
drawn as two upright walls under a brown roof slab with round ends that overhangs both sides, a blue
window with soft corners, a rod leaning from the deck up to the left, and a fishing line dropping
straight from the rod's tip to a small J hook. Traces clean. Cut the hull at the stern's top corner and
the bow's tip. The rod runs from the deck up; the fishing line runs from the rod's tip down. Rod and
line meet at a sharp tip: zoom in there.)

- intro: "Early morning, calm water, one rod over the side. This little fishing boat has a cabin to keep the rain off. Watch it get ready, then cast your own line."
- deck: "Start with the deck: a level line that curves up at the right into a pointed bow."
- hull: "Hang the hull under it. From the left end, curve down, along the bottom and up to the point."
- plank: "One plank line goes along the side, a little shorter than the hull."
- cabin: "The cabin begins as two upright walls on the deck."
- roof: "Lay a flat roof across the walls. It's a slab with round ends, wider than the cabin."
- window: "Give the cabin a square window with soft corners."
- rod: "The fishing rod leans out over the stern. One straight line from the deck, up and to the left."
- line: "Drop the fishing line straight down from the rod's tip. Finish with a tiny hook, like a letter J."
- colors: "Color the hull dark green." / "Color the cabin yellow." / "Color the roof brown and the window blue."
- outro: "The line is in the water. Now all you need is patience."

### submarine

(Picture: yellow capsule with long flat top and bottom and round ends, an orange tower with a rounded
top standing on the middle, a bold periscope rising from the tower and turning right, three blue
portholes in a row. Traces clean; portholes touch nothing. The body is about 2,000 around: cut it at
the middle of each round end into a top half and a bottom half, both drawn left to right. If the
periscope came as two pieces, join them so it is one line from the tower up and over.)

- intro: "Dive, dive, dive. A submarine is a capsule with round windows, and a periscope to peek above the waves. Watch it surface, then take yours down."
- body-top: "A submarine is a long capsule. From the middle left, arch up, along the top and down the far end."
- body-bottom: "Go back to the left. Draw the same shape underneath, like a reflection in water."
- tower: "The tower sits on top, in the middle: up, over a rounded top, and down."
- periscope: "The periscope rises from the tower, then turns to look ahead. It's an upside-down letter L."
- portholes: "Three round portholes in a row. Draw the middle one first, then one on each side."
- colors: "Color the body yellow." / "Color the tower orange." / "Color the portholes blue."
- outro: "Periscope up, three portholes in a row. What do you think is down there?"

### cruise-ship

(Picture: long blue hull with a stripe, its deck line sweeping up to the bow; a cream lower deck with
five square windows and a sloped front holding one slanted blue window; a shorter yellow upper deck
with four windows; a red funnel, narrower at the top, with a gray band between two lines. Seventeen
lines, all clean. Cut the hull at the stern's top corner and the bow's tip. The lower deck's line goes up the back and along its top; the upper deck is one line; the bridge's
sloped front is its own line, drawn from the upper deck's front corner (the picture has no ink between the two decks there). Blue is one area: hull and all ten windows.
Twelve steps is the ceiling, so the bridge window shares a step with the upper windows.)

- intro: "All aboard. This ship has two decks of windows stacked on a long blue hull, and a striped funnel on top. Watch the decks pile up, then set sail yourself."
- deck: "A cruise ship is a floating hotel. Begin with the hull's top: a long line sweeping up to the bow."
- hull: "Go down the stern and straight along the bottom. Then climb to the bow's tip."
- stripe: "Draw one long stripe along the hull."
- lower-deck: "The first deck stands on the hull. Go up the back and along its flat top."
- upper-deck: "Stack a shorter deck on top. From its front corner, step out and slope down to the hull."
- funnel: "The funnel stands on the top deck, narrower at its top. Two lines across make a band."
- windows-lower: "Five square windows go along the lower deck. Keep them level and evenly spaced."
- windows-upper: "Four more on the upper deck. Then the captain's window, slanting with the front slope."
- colors: "Color the hull and every window blue." / "Color the lower deck cream." / "Color the upper deck yellow." / "The funnel is red, with a gray band."
- outro: "Nine windows, a bridge and a funnel. Which cabin would you pick?"

### pirate-ship

(Picture: brown hull with a raised block at the stern, a step down, and a deck running out to a
rounded bow; one plank line; a bowsprit line from the bow; a main mast with a large cream sail and a
plain red flag streaming left from its top; a smaller front mast and sail. Each sail is flat on top and
bottom, with both sides curving toward the bow. Masts show as short pieces below and above the sails.
Nine lines: several run two things together (flag with mast top, hull with bowsprit): cut them so
hull, bowsprit, each mast piece, each sail and the flag are their own lines. Cut the hull at the
stern's top-left corner and at the bow. Mast pieces rise; the flag starts on the mast.)

- intro: "Ahoy. Every pirate story needs a ship: a big wooden hull, two sails full of wind, and a flag on the tallest mast. Watch her get rigged, then hoist your own sails."
- deck: "Begin at the back of the ship. Draw the high stern, step down, and run the deck to the bow."
- hull: "Now the big wooden belly. From the stern, curve down, along and up to the bow."
- details: "Add a plank line along the side. Then a pole pointing out from the bow."
- main-post: "The main mast stands in the middle of the deck. For now, draw just a short post."
- main-sail: "Hang the main sail on the post. Flat on top and bottom, with both sides bulging toward the bow."
- flag: "The mast carries on above the sail. Fly a small flag from its top, streaming back."
- front-mast: "Build the front mast the same way, only smaller: a post, a sail, and a tip on top."
- colors: "Color the hull brown." / "Color both sails cream." / "Color the flag red."
- outro: "Sails full, flag flying. Where is the treasure buried?"

### longship

(Picture: brown hull curved like a smile, a short stern tip on the left, a tall prow on the right
ending in a spiral scroll drawn as one line; five round shields along the side, red, yellow, red,
yellow, red; a mast piece below and above a wide sail with bulging sides; four lines from the sail's
top to its bottom making five stripes, red, cream, red, cream, red. Thirteen lines, clean; the shields
touch nothing. The hull is long: cut it at the stern's tip and at the prow's tip (where the scroll
begins) into a top curve and a bottom curve, both left to right. The scroll runs from the prow's tip
inward. Red is one area: three stripes and three shields.)

- intro: "Long ago, ships like this crossed cold northern seas with one striped sail and a row of shields along the side. See the scroll curl at the prow, then sail yours."
- hull-top: "A longship is one long curve. From the stern's tip, dip low and sweep up the tall prow."
- scroll: "The prow ends in a scroll. Curl the line around itself, like a cinnamon roll."
- hull-bottom: "Back at the stern, draw the keel: a deeper curve underneath, all the way to the prow."
- shields: "Five round shields hang along the side. Draw them in a row that follows the curve."
- mast: "The mast stands in the middle of the ship. Draw a short post and leave room for the sail."
- sail: "The sail is wide, with bulging sides. Draw its outline, then the mast's tip above it."
- stripes: "Four lines from top to bottom split the sail into five stripes."
- colors: "Color the hull brown." / "Color three stripes and three shields red." / "Color the two stripes in between cream." / "The last two shields are yellow."
- outro: "Five shields, five stripes, one proud scroll. Your longship is ready to explore."

### harbour

(The id keeps its planned spelling; every word a learner reads or hears says "harbor". Picture:
a lighthouse in the middle on a gray rock mound: leaning tower with two band lines, red, cream, red, a
yellow lantern drawn as two short walls, a red pointed roof; a small sailboat on the left, blue hull,
mast, cream sail; a small tugboat on the right, red hull, gray bumper, yellow cabin, round blue
window, orange stack; five waves below, three in a row and two under them, each with two humps. Twenty
lines, clean; small shapes stay whole. Seven outline steps and five color steps: twelve is the ceiling.
Red, cream, blue, yellow and gray each cover parts of two things, and the color steps say so. Waves
run left to right.)

- intro: "Here's where every boat comes home. A lighthouse on its rock, a sailboat, a tugboat, and waves rolling in. You've drawn these boats before, so watch once, then bring them into port."
- rock: "A harbor needs a lighthouse, and a lighthouse needs a rock. Draw a low mound with a flat bottom."
- tower: "The tower stands on the rock and leans in as it rises. Two lines across make three bands."
- lantern: "On top sits the lantern: two short walls, and a pointed roof like a little hat."
- sailboat: "On the left, a sailboat. Draw its bowl of a hull, a mast, and a triangle sail."
- tug-hull: "On the right, a tugboat. Draw the long bumper first, then the tub of a hull under it."
- tug-cabin: "Build the tug's cabin, its round window, and a smokestack on the roof."
- waves: "Last, the waves. Each is a wiggle with two humps: three in a row, then two below."
- colors: "Color two bands, the roof and the tug's hull red." / "Color the middle band and the sail cream." / "Color the sailboat's hull and the tug's window blue." / "Color the lantern and the tug's cabin yellow." / "Color the rock and the bumper gray, and the smokestack orange."
- outro: "The light is on and the boats are safe in the harbor. You started with one sail, and now you have a whole scene."
