# Forms: what Lina says

The words of a path are planned together, so ten lessons in a row do not say the same sentence ten
times, and do not sound like ten different teachers either. This is the plan for the ten `forms`
lessons (Core level, 2026-09-20). The models to follow are the creator's own Strawberry and the
scripts in [fruits-words.md](fruits-words.md) and [food-treats-words.md](food-treats-words.md).

Forms is the skills path, so Lina names the idea each lesson teaches, in a child's words: a box has
three faces, a circle seen from above is a flat oval, light comes from the top left, the side away
from the light is darker, a shadow falls away from the light. One idea per lesson, said once.

## Rules

1. **US English.** Color, center, gray.
2. **Say what is being drawn, first.** "The top is a diamond lying flat." "Now the upright edges."
   A step never opens with only "Start at the top". Then, if it helps, where the pen starts and one
   familiar picture (a wide smile, a letter V, a letter C, a step).
3. **One or two short sentences, under about twelve words each, 110 characters in all.** Calm, no
   exclamation marks inside steps, no praise, no jargon, no sizes. Say "oval", not "ellipse"; "edge"
   and "side", not "plane"; "slanted lines", not "hatching" (the still life may say "hatch" once,
   after the sphere has shown what it is).
4. **Link steps the way a teacher talks:** "Now", "Next", "Then", "One more". About one step in three.
5. **No sentence is shared word for word with another lesson of any path, except a plain color
   step.** The forms repeat on purpose (four lessons have an oval rim, two sides and a sagging
   bottom), so each script below words them differently; keep those differences.
6. **Color steps name the thing and the color.** Plain is the default; at most one per lesson says
   why ("The right side is away from the light. Make it dark green."). Lina says "light green",
   "green", "dark green", "black" for the charcoal pips, "pink" for watermelon pink.
7. **Intros each open differently and end by handing over, in different words.** Outros are one or
   two short sentences about their drawing, never about getting it right.
8. **Titles stay short and plain:** "Draw the top", "Draw the uprights", "Color the left side".
   Titles may repeat across lessons; they are labels, not speech.
9. **Drawing rules still hold** (see the author-lesson skill): straight edges stay whole; a big
   closed curve (the ball, a wide oval rim) is split in two where a pen would lift; small closed
   shapes (a pip, the tab, the bow's knot) stay whole; one thing is finished before the next; at
   most six lines in a step.

## Phrases already used by published lessons (do not reuse)

- Openers: "Today we're drawing …", "In this lesson we're drawing …", "Hi, Lina here. Ready to draw
  …?", "Here comes a summer favorite …", "Let's cut an orange in half …", "Grapes are just circles
  …", "A pineapple looks tricky, but …", "You know these fruits already.", "Who wants a donut?", "On
  a hot day …", "Two scoops, please.", "It's somebody's birthday somewhere …", "Pizza night.",
  "Fries taste best …", "Let's stack a burger …", "Boba tea has …", "A bowl of ramen has a lot going
  on …", "Lunch is served."
- Hand-overs: "Watch how it comes together, then it's your turn." (retired), "Watch the whole thing
  once, then grab your pen.", "See how the slice is built, then you draw it.", "… then pick up your
  pen.", "… then it's over to you.", "… then you try.", "… then make one of your own.", "… then draw
  your own.", "… then it's your go.", "… then you're the baker.", "… then the pen is yours.", "…
  then fill a carton of your own.", "… then build yours.", "… then draw one for yourself.", "… then
  start your own bowl.", "… then serve up your own."
- Step sentences: "Start at the top. Draw a big letter C down to the bottom.", "Go back to the top.
  Draw the same curve the other way, like a mirror.", "Draw one line along the middle of the leaf.
  Stop before the tip.", "First the peel, a big circle. From the top, curve round the left to the
  bottom.", "Back at the top, curve round the right. The circle closes at the bottom.", "Close the
  slice with a curved bottom, like a wide smile.", "Begin with the bowl. Draw its front rim, a long
  curve like a smile."
- Outros: "You did it. That's your very own …", "There they are …", "There it is …", "And that's a
  … Hold it up and have a good look. You made that!", "That's your …", "One … all yours.", "What a
  bunch.", "That's a fine … you've drawn.", "A whole bowl of fruit …", "One donut, iced and
  sprinkled by you.", "Tray's up."

## The ten scripts

Step ids and grouping follow the trace; where a lesson's trace gives a different grouping, keep the
voice of these lines and adapt the count. Anything changed is reported back, and all ten are read
side by side before the creator reviews them.

### cube

(Picture: a hexagon outline with a Y inside, sharp corners, three greens. The trace gives the
hexagon as one closed line: cut it at its six corners so every edge is its own straight line. Nine
lines, drawn top first.)

- intro: "Every box you'll ever draw starts here, with a cube. It's only nine straight lines. Watch where each one goes, then draw your own cube."
- top-back: "The top is a diamond lying flat. From the far corner, draw one edge out to each side."
- top-front: "Close the diamond. Bring both edges in to meet at the near corner."
- uprights: "Now the upright edges. Drop a straight line from the left, near and right corners."
- base: "Join the bottoms with two edges. Each runs the same way as the top edge above it."
- colors: "Color the top light green." / "Color the left side green." / "The right side is away from the light. Make it dark green."
- outro: "Nine lines, three faces, one solid cube. It looks like you could pick it up."

### cylinder

(Second picture, 2026-09-20, bold lines: a whole oval on top, two sides, a sagging bottom.)

- intro: "A cylinder is a tin can with nothing on it: an oval on top, two straight sides, a curve below. See how they join, then have a go."
- top-back: "A circle seen from the side looks like a flat oval. Draw its back, from the left end over to the right."
- top-front: "Come back along the front to close the oval."
- sides: "Draw a straight side down from each end of the oval. Make them match."
- bottom: "Join the sides with a sagging curve. It copies the front of the oval."
- colors: "Color the body orange." / "Color the top yellow." (largest area first)
- outro: "An oval, two sides and a curve, and it's standing on your page. That's a cylinder."

### cone

(Second picture, 2026-09-20, bold lines: three lines. Four steps is the fewest a lesson may have,
so the color is its own step.)

- intro: "Think of a party hat. A cone is only three lines, and the trick is the curved base. Look how they meet, then it's your pen's turn."
- base: "Begin with the base. It's the front of a flat oval, sagging in the middle."
- left: "Now the left side. From the left end of the curve, lean a straight line up to the tip."
- right: "The right side leans the other way. Draw it up to meet the first at the tip."
- color: "Color the cone purple."
- outro: "Three lines, and it stands up by itself. Add stripes if you'd like a party hat."

### sphere

(Picture: a circle, five slanted hatch lines on its lower right, an outlined gray shadow whose left
end is hidden by the ball. The circle is split in two halves; the hatch lines go three, then two.)

- intro: "A circle is flat. Give it a shadow and it turns into a ball. The light here comes from the top left. Watch the dark side appear, then try it."
- ball-left: "The ball starts as a big circle. From the top, swing around the left side to where it rests."
- ball-right: "From the top again, swing around the right side and close the ball."
- hatch-top: "Light can't reach the lower right. Draw three slanted lines there, side by side."
- hatch-bottom: "Add two more slanted lines below them. That's the ball's dark side."
- shadow: "The ball blocks the light, so a shadow falls to the right. Draw a long flat oval from under the ball."
- colors: "Color the ball orange." / "Color the shadow gray."
- outro: "A few slanted lines and a shadow, and your circle became a ball. Light did that."

### dice

(Picture: a cube with softly rounded corners, cream, yellow and orange, six solid pips: one, two
and three. Drawn outside first this time, then the Y inside, so it is not the cube lesson again.)

- intro: "Roll the dice. This one is a cube with dots, and each dot leans the same way as the face it sits on. Watch closely, then roll your own."
- outline-top: "Begin with the outside. From the far corner, draw an edge sloping down to each side."
- outline-sides: "From both ends, draw a straight side going down."
- outline-bottom: "Bring two edges in from the sides. They meet at the bottom corner."
- top-front: "Now the front of the top. Draw a wide letter V from corner to corner."
- middle: "From the point of the V, draw one line straight down. There are your three faces."
- pips-top: "One dot goes on top. Make it a squashed oval, lying flat like the top."
- pips-left: "Two dots on the left face, one high and one low. They lean with the face."
- pips-right: "Three dots go on the right face in a slanted row. They lean the other way."
- colors: "Color the top cream." / "Color the left face yellow." / "The right face is orange." / "Fill in all six dots black."
  (Twelve steps at most: if the count runs over, put the two outline steps "sides" and "bottom"
  together as "From both ends, draw a side going down, then bring two edges in to the bottom corner.")
- outro: "One, two, three. Your dice is ready to roll, and every dot sits flat on its face."

### gift-box

(Picture: box and lid in gray, blue and purple, ribbon bands in cream, yellow and orange, a bow of
two loops and a knot. The heaviest lesson of the path: 31 lines, all twelve steps used. As built:
the bow hides the middle of both back edges of the lid and one edge of each back band, the lid's
front edges run whole across the ribbon, and the trace gives the bow's loops the left band's yellow
and the knot the right band's orange, so they are colored together.)

- intro: "Somebody is getting a present. It's a cube wearing a lid, a ribbon and a bow. There are lots of lines, all easy ones. Watch the wrapping, then wrap yours."
- bow: "We start with the bow. Draw a small circle for the knot. Add a fat loop on each side."
- lid-top: "Behind the bow is the lid's top, a flat diamond. Draw its edges around the bow."
- lid-sides: "Give the lid some depth. Draw three short lines down, one at each corner."
- lid-bottom: "Now join them along the bottom. The ribbon leaves a gap on each side."
- box: "The box sits under the lid. Draw three long uprights, then two edges along the bottom."
- ribbon-top: "Now the ribbon. From the bow, two lines run to each front edge. One goes to each back edge."
- ribbon-left: "Carry the ribbon down the left side with two straight lines."
- ribbon-right: "Do the same down the right side. The ribbon goes all the way around."
- colors: "Color the lid's top gray." / "Color the left sides blue and the right sides purple." / "The ribbon is cream where it crosses the top." / "The bands are yellow on the left, orange on the right. The bow is yellow, its knot orange."
- outro: "All wrapped up, with a bow on top. Who is your present for?"

### soda-can

(Picture: a plain red can with a cream band, a blue rim ring, a gray lid and a blue tab; the model
gave it a small sloped shoulder under the rim, which stays.)

- intro: "Psst. That's the sound of a can opening. A soda can is a cylinder with a few extras on it. Watch them go on, then draw a can of your own."
- rim-back: "The rim comes first, a flat oval. Curve over the back from left to right."
- rim-front: "Swing back along the front to finish the rim."
- ring: "Draw a second oval just inside the first. Now the rim is a thin ring."
- tab: "Add the pull tab, a small rounded shape lying on the lid."
- sides: "Now the sides. From each end of the rim, slope out a little, then go straight down."
- bottom: "Close the can with a curve. It sags the same way as the rim."
- band: "The label is a band. Draw two curves across the middle, sagging like the bottom."
- colors: "Color the can red, above and below the band." / "Color the band cream." / "The lid is gray." / "Color the ring and the tab blue."
- outro: "A cold can of soda with a label waiting for a name. What flavor is inside?"

### mug

(Second picture, 2026-09-20, bold lines: rim oval, inner oval, two sides, bottom curve, a C-shaped
handle on the left.)

- intro: "A mug of hot chocolate is a short, wide cylinder with a handle to hold on to. Watch it take shape, then pour one for yourself."
- rim-back: "Begin at the rim, a wide flat oval. Draw its back curve first."
- rim-front: "Then its front curve, from one end to the other."
- inner: "Draw a smaller oval inside, back curve then front. The gap is the mug's thick edge."
- sides: "Drop a straight side from each end of the rim."
- bottom: "Round off the bottom with one sagging curve."
- handle-outer: "The handle is a letter C, backwards. Draw its outside curve from the mug's side and back again."
- handle-inner: "Draw a smaller curve inside it. That's the hole for your fingers."
- colors: "Color the mug and its handle red." / "Color the thick edge pink." / "Fill the mug with brown hot chocolate."
- outro: "One warm mug, filled right to the top. Careful, it's hot."

### stack-of-books

(Picture kept by the creator, 2026-09-20: TWO books, not three, the smaller one resting on the
larger, soft rounded spines, one page line on each. Traced with `svg from-image --min-area 10` so
the thin page lines survive; their stray specks are deleted.)

- intro: "A book is a flat box, and you can already draw a box. We'll rest a small one on a big one. Watch the pile grow, then stack your own."
- top-cover: "Begin with the top book, since nothing hides it. Draw its cover, a flat slanted diamond."
- top-spine: "Give it thickness. Draw the rounded spine down the left, then along under the cover."
- top-pages: "On the right, the pages. Draw a short line down, then along the bottom and back up."
- bottom-cover: "The big book peeks out below. Draw the strip of cover that shows, like a step."
- bottom-spine: "Then its spine. Round the left end and run along the bottom."
- bottom-pages: "Close its page side on the right, the same way as the book above."
- pages: "Draw one line along each book's pages."
- colors: "Color the covers blue on top and green below." / "Color the spines purple and dark green." / "Color all the pages cream."
- outro: "Two books, neatly piled. What would their titles be?"

### still-life

(Second picture, 2026-09-20: mug on the left, ball in the middle, two books on the right, three
hatch lines on the ball. The shadows have no dark outline in the picture, so their outlines come
from the color-edge trace, cut where they meet the thing that casts them.)

- intro: "Artists call a group of objects a still life. Ours has a mug, a ball and two books, all lit from the top left. Watch where the shadows fall, then set up your own."
- mug-rim: "On the left goes the mug. Draw the oval rim and the smaller oval inside it."
- mug-body: "Add the two sides, the curved bottom, and the handle on the left."
- ball: "Next to it, the ball. Draw its circle in two halves, left then right."
- ball-hatch: "Its lower right is turned from the light. Hatch it with three slanted lines."
- book-top: "On the right, two books. Start with the small one on top: cover, spine, pages."
- book-bottom: "Tuck the big book under it. Draw its strip of cover, its spine and its pages."
- (no page-line step: the still life's page lines are too faint to trace, so the books go without)
- shadows-round: "Every shadow falls to the right. Draw a curved one beside the mug and one beside the ball."
- shadow-books: "The books cast a thin slanted strip. Draw it along the ground."
- colors (three steps at most, to stay within twelve): "Color the mug red, its edge pink, the drink brown and the ball orange." / "Color the books: blue and purple on top, greens below, cream pages." / "Color all three shadows gray."
- outro: "A mug, a ball and books, all under one light. That's a real still life, and that's the Forms path done."
